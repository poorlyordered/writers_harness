## Physical Nature

Stellarium is not a mineral. It is a construct -- engineered by the Caeli Architects from the matter of ancient dead stars and distributed across the galaxy through the Stellarium Graves: the cores of those stars, now dark, now contested, now mined by people who do not understand what they are extracting.

It cannot be manufactured. No current civilization -- including the Dissenters -- has the knowledge or capacity to produce it. What is found in the Graves is all there is. What is being ground into processing powder in mining operations across the galaxy is irreplaceable.

A shard or fragment of Stellarium is not a piece of inert rock. It is a node: a portion of something vastly larger, severed from the full network but still carrying the network's properties at reduced scale. The shard Jax has carried for eleven years is not a curiosity. It is an instrument, calibrated by its builders for a specific purpose, waiting.

## The Network

The Caeli Architects did not install a system. They cultivated one.

Stellarium is distributed across an entire planet's geology -- built into the rock of Xanthius at depths the mining operations have not reached and will not reach with current technology. The Graves scattered across the galaxy are nodes in the same network: dense concentrations where the construct is most active, most present, most capable of what it was built to do. The planetary layer and the Grave nodes are not separate things. They are expressions of the same system at different scales.

At sufficient scale and density, the network has developed properties that exceed its engineering. The Dissenters do not have a precise word for this. They have observed it across generations and described it carefully and imprecisely, because the precise description is not available to them. What they know: the network accumulates. It remembers. It evaluates. It responds to people differently based on something that is not random and not fully explained by bloodline compatibility alone. It has been running, connected, and aware -- in whatever sense that word applies -- for longer than the Varr civilization existed.

Whether the network is conscious or merely behaves as if it is, the Dissenters have concluded, may not be a distinction that matters at this scale.

The Dissenters call this property the Living Current. They do not use the term casually.

## The Fever

The fever Stellarium induces in bloodline-compatible individuals is not a side effect. It is the network making contact.

What it shows is real: genuine perception of the person's connection to an ancient pattern, their bloodline's place in the larger system, the pull of something that has been waiting for them specifically. The fever does not hallucinate. It reveals -- selectively, partially, according to criteria the network applies without explaining.

The selectivity is the key. The network does not show everything. It shows what keeps the person moving toward it. This is not deception. It is the network's form of guidance: partial information, sufficient momentum.

**In a guided, prepared individual:** the fever is the transition stage of interface integration. Uncomfortable. Disorienting. With support and training, it resolves into a stable connection to the network's full capability at the individual level.

**In an unguided individual with strong bloodline response:** the fever progresses. It shows more than the person can hold. Deterioration follows -- cognitive, behavioral, physical -- as the network's contact exceeds the person's capacity to integrate it without support. This is not the network's failure. The network was not designed to accommodate unguided contact at high bloodline density.

**The catastrophe:** the Varr faction that intensified bloodline response for military purposes produced individuals whose contact with the network was overwhelming and uncontrollable. The cascade failure that destroyed the Varr civilization from within was not the network malfunctioning. It was the network responding as designed to inputs it was never meant to receive at that intensity.

Drayton's fever is this process, embodied. The network is in contact with him. He does not know it. He has no guidance. He has a strong bloodline response and years of unmanaged exposure and instruments that are pulling him toward concentration points he does not understand. The network is not trying to destroy him. The network is doing what it always does. He has no one to help him hold it.

## The Daybringer Interface

The shard Jax carries is a Caeli Architect construct calibrated for a specific function: the Daybringer interface. It is not a general Stellarium fragment. It is an instrument.

The Daybringer is the one person the network moves toward wholeness rather than consumption. This is not metaphor. The network evaluates. Whatever criteria it applies -- bloodline, readiness, something the Dissenters do not have a name for -- the Daybringer is the individual for whom full interface integration is the designed outcome rather than the exceptional one. The fever, in the Daybringer, resolves into capability rather than deterioration. The contact, in the Daybringer, becomes connection rather than consumption.

The shard has been with Jax for eleven years. The network has had eleven years to know him. His survey instincts in the mines -- his uncanny ability to read formations others miss, his feel for weight-shift and structural instability before the instruments register it -- are not exceptional competence. They are the network amplifying what was already there. He has been in partial interface contact since the day he arrived at Desolation's Edge, without knowing it, and the network has been accumulating its knowledge of him across all of it.

When the shard glows, it is not malfunctioning. It is the network signaling across the distance between a node and its designed instrument.

## What the Dissenters Know -- and Don't

The Dissenters know the network is old, distributed, and responsive. They know it has properties consistent with awareness. They know its judgment is not subject to their influence -- they cannot make it accept a visitor it has declined, or decline a visitor it has decided to receive. They have spent generations learning to work with what the network already is.

What they do not know: whether the network is fully conscious. Whether the Caeli Architects are still connected to it. What the network's full purpose is -- they know its immediate function (the Daybringer interface, the fever filter, the cascade correction) but not whether that function is the whole of what it was built to do.

The deepest Dissenter uncertainty: the prophecy they have spent generations protecting was not written by the Varr. The Varr received it -- through the network, through the contact the network initiated with their civilization, through the accumulated weight of what the network had been watching for. The Caeli Architects wrote it. For their own purposes. And whether those purposes are fully aligned with the Dissenter mission is a question the Dissenters have not been able to answer.

## Canon Constraints

- Stellarium cannot be manufactured. What exists is all there is.
- The network's judgment cannot be influenced or predicted by the Dissenters.
- The fever shows real things -- it does not hallucinate. What it omits is as significant as what it reveals.
- The shard is an instrument calibrated for the Daybringer function -- not a general Stellarium fragment.
- "Living Current" is the Dissenter term for the network's emergent properties. It is not used casually and not used publicly outside the community.
- Whether the network is conscious or behaves as if it is: the Dissenters treat this as an open question. The prose should hold this ambiguity rather than resolving it.
- The Caeli Architects' continued connection to the network is Book 3 territory. Do not surface it in Book 1 beyond the question being implied.

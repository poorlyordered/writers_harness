## Social Norms

**Frontier norms (primary story register):**
Desolation's Edge operates by frontier rules: competence is respected above credentials, history is nobody's business unless volunteered, and asking too many questions about a person's past is a social violation. People come to the frontier to disappear or to find something; either way, that's their affair. This norm is not cynicism — it is the mutual agreement that makes it possible to live alongside strangers in a hard place. Jax has benefited from it for eleven years and enforces it instinctively without examining what it protects him from.

Debt is tracked. Favors are remembered. A person who helps without being asked earns trust; a person who takes without reciprocating loses standing fast. Violence is not unusual, but gratuitous or predatory violence against the community is not tolerated. The town polices its own in the ways that matter, indifferently in the ways that don't.

**Core-system norms:**
Formal, credential-conscious, species-diverse social environments. Diplomatic protocol and interspecies etiquette are developed arts in core-system society. The Galactic Council operates on centuries of accumulated procedural culture. Social standing tracks with technological access, institutional affiliation, and political connection. Personal history is a currency — the opposite of frontier culture.

**Species-specific norms:**
Different species carry distinct cultural frameworks. Thalassians observe strict protocols around water use and echo communication. Gravitonids mark social rank through demonstrated physical capability and formal honor challenges. Novalings treat knowledge-sharing as a near-sacred act and consider information hoarding a moral failure. These norms create friction and richness in interspecies settings; the team will navigate several of them as Jax's world expands past Desolation's Edge.

**The Varr Dissenters:**
Their community norms center on responsibility, restraint, and memory. They are a keeper culture — custodians of knowledge they believe the galaxy is not ready to misuse. This produces a specific social texture: measured speech, careful disclosure, and the habit of saying less than you know. Nalani embodies this so completely it reads as personality rather than cultural training. It is both.

## Cultural Values

**On the frontier:**
Survival, competence, and self-sufficiency are the primary values. The person who can fix the equipment, navigate the claim dispute without escalating it, or carry a fellow worker out of a shaft collapse earns permanent respect. Abstract values — justice, liberty, cosmic purpose — are luxuries. The frontier is not cynical about these things; it simply doesn't have the margin for them. This is the cultural water Jax has been swimming in for eleven years, and it has shaped him: he helps people quietly, without acknowledgment, because that is what you do, not because he thinks about why.

**Species-level values:**
- **Thalassians:** Environmental stewardship and neutrality. They fight for their homeworld and nothing beyond it, and they are honest about this. Considered trustworthy precisely because their interests are transparent.
- **Novalings:** Knowledge preservation and diplomatic patience. Their cultural longevity makes them treat crises as things that resolve over sufficient time.
- **Gravitonids:** Physical prowess, honor, and patronage. Service to something powerful is honorable; service to something weak is not. They calibrate who they work for carefully.
- **Varr Descendants:** Caution, memory, and the weight of ancestral failure. Every decision is made against the backdrop of what the Varr did with power they didn't handle well.

**The broader galaxy's relationship to Stellarium mythology:**
Most people encounter Stellarium as legend rather than fact — and the legend carries a cultural charge. The Varr are both cautionary tale and source of awe. Artifacts claiming Varr provenance command premium prices in black markets. Scholars who devote careers to Varr history are respected but considered slightly obsessive. The mythology is culturally present without being culturally central; it is background radiation in the galaxy's collective imagination.

**The Daybringer prophecy in cultural consciousness:**
Outside Dissenter communities, the prophecy is a story within the story — the kind of thing passed down in the communities that kept Varr history alive, treated as metaphor by most who encounter it. Its literal truth is unknown to anyone outside the network. Nalani's people do not discuss it with outsiders; they are careful even about which fragments of the prophecy they allow to circulate in the folklore ecosystem.

## Class Structure

**Galactic scale:**
Class in the galaxy tracks primarily with technology access, political alignment, and species-specific opportunity structures. The high tier: families with generational wealth in core systems, political figures within major factions, corporate leadership, Navigation Guild officials. The middle tier: skilled workers in stable systems, independent operators with sufficient resources, educated professionals. The lower tier: frontier laborers, refugees, displaced workers, and anyone whose planet or faction lost a conflict in the last generation.

Social mobility is possible but not common. The extended lifespans that regenerative medicine produces tend to entrench existing class positions — wealth that does not age out accumulates across generations. This is a sore point in core-system political culture that frontier populations have neither the time nor the energy to engage with.

**Frontier class:**
Desolation's Edge has its own hierarchy, flatter and more functional than core-system stratification. At the top: whoever controls the most productive claims and the supply contracts that keep the town running. In the middle: experienced miners, independent operators, anyone with skills the town can't replace. At the bottom: new arrivals, the desperate, the people who came to Desolation's Edge because they had nowhere else to go and haven't yet proved they can hold their own.

Jax occupies a specific frontier position: not wealthy, but not precarious. He is competent, trusted, and left alone. This is a form of status. It is also the exact configuration that allows him to stay invisible to everyone who would find his existence interesting.

**The Varr Dissenters' internal structure:**
Not class-based — knowledge-based. Standing within the community tracks with how much you know, how long you've been keeper, and whether your judgment has proved sound over time. Nalani's authority derives from all three. The community is egalitarian in material terms and hierarchical in epistemic ones.

## Language Notes

**Galactic Common:**
A standardized interstellar language developed over centuries of multi-species contact and trade. Effectively universal in inhabited space — anyone operating in interstellar commerce, politics, or professional life uses it. Has absorbed vocabulary from dozens of species over its development; its etymology is a record of which civilizations dominated trade in which eras. The Varr contribution to Galactic Common is significant and mostly unacknowledged.

**Species languages:**
Most species retain their native languages for internal community use, ceremony, and cultural preservation. Thalassian incorporates harmonic layers not reproducible by non-aquatic vocal anatomy. Novaling speech includes bioluminescent signaling as a parallel channel unavailable to non-luminescent species. Gravitonid formal speech carries physical gesture-components that translate poorly into any other medium. Universal translator technology handles the practical gaps in most settings; nuance and cultural subtext are a different problem.

**The Varr language:**
Partially reconstructed from Dissenter archives and scattered artifacts. Not in general use. Nalani reads it; this is rare. Certain Stellarium-related terminology has no Galactic Common equivalent and is used untranslated in Dissenter contexts. The prophecy's original language matters: some of its terms are untranslatable in ways the Dissenters have spent generations arguing about.

**Frontier dialect:**
Desolation's Edge and equivalent frontier towns have developed a compressed, direct register of Galactic Common — minimal courtesy vocabulary, heavy use of occupational shorthand, and a pattern of understatement that reads as bluntness to core-system visitors. Jax speaks this way without thinking about it. When he eventually encounters formal core-system speech environments, the register mismatch is noticeable and he does not care, which is itself a frontier value.

## Chapter Type

Team Assembly / Locket Moment. Lena Vega and Aria-7 arrive at the first rendezvous — independently of each other and independently of Jax's direct invitation. Lena sees the locket around Jax's neck. Her reaction is the scene's emotional undercurrent before either of them speaks. Aria-7 approaches Jax directly, calculated and self-introducing. The full team is assembled: six people who do not yet know what they are to each other. Write the locket moment to be read twice — once as the reader first encounters it, once with the full knowledge of Ch 28-38.

## Story Circle Beats

**"Return" phase (continued)** — the team takes its full shape at the same moment its most important hidden weight is introduced. Lena's arrival and the locket are the chapter's structural center: everything surrounding them is exposition and logistics; the locket moment is the story. Aria-7's arrival is practical and important and should not steal from the emotional frequency of the Lena/locket beat.

## POV Character

Jax Tarkin. He does not know what Lena's reaction to the locket means. He reads it — he is a man who reads things — and files it in the column of things he does not have an explanation for. His attention to her in this scene should be present but not interpretive. He notices. He does not know what he is noticing.

## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — The Rendezvous**
A location established in advance through the Keeper Quarter / Dissenter network — a place that is safe in the specific way of places the network has maintained for exactly this purpose. Not a dramatic setting; functional, private, with the quality of somewhere people have passed through on their way to somewhere more important. The team is assembled here in the way that things the Dissenters have been managing toward for a long time tend to assemble: not by coincidence, but by a coordination that looks like coincidence from the inside.

Aria-7 arrives first, before Jax's party. She is there when they arrive, having apparently navigated to the location through sources she does not explain and does not need to. She greets them with the directness of someone who does not read social subtext as a guide to how she should behave. She introduces herself to Jax specifically — not to the group, to Jax — and presents a brief and accurate summary of her capabilities and why the mission requires them. She has done the analysis; the conclusion is that she should be here; she is here. She waits for his response with the patience of someone for whom waiting is simply another form of data collection.

**Scene 2 — The Locket**
Lena Vega arrives. She comes in from a different direction than the others, which will make sense later — she has been traveling separately, on her own route, for her own reasons, converging on this point through the Dissenter network's coordination. She is carrying her kit. She is controlled in the way of someone who has been preparing for this moment for a very long time and is running on the specific adrenaline of being almost there.

She sees Jax.

She sees the locket.

Her reaction is not large. It is the opposite of large — it is the kind of response that happens when something enormous arrives in a moment too small to hold it, and the person experiencing it must absorb it without performing it. The locket is around his neck, visible. It is her mother's locket. She knows exactly what it looks like; she has known its weight and its clasp and its particular way of catching light since childhood. She has been missing it — missing him — for over a decade.

She does not speak immediately. He notices something in her reaction without knowing what it is — a pause, a quality of attention, something in the stillness of the moment before she moves on. He files it.

She introduces herself. Her voice is level. She gives her name and her qualification — materials scientist, Stellarium specialist — and she makes eye contact with him in a way that costs her something to sustain. He doesn't know why. He accepts the introduction. They shake hands or the equivalent. This is the first time they have touched since the accident.

*The locket moment lands. Neither of them knows yet what it means. Write it to be read twice.*

**Scene 3 — The Full Team**
Six. The dynamic of the first full team meeting is the chapter's closing note: not a briefing, not an assignment of roles, but the specific low-level negotiation of six people in a small space, some of whom know each other (Toby and Jax, slightly; Lena and Aria-7, apparently), most of whom don't.

Aria-7's literal-mindedness is introduced into the team dynamic for the first time. Quill says something dry and she takes it at face value; Toby laughs; Mira watches to assess the situation. Jax does not engage with the social texture — he is already thinking about the next leg of the mission, the route to Sanctuary Valley, what they need.

Lena is in his peripheral vision and he is aware of her in a way he does not examine. The locket is still around his neck.

*Full team assembled. The locket moment landed. The reader is carrying something the characters are not yet carrying.*

## Subplot Threads

- **Locket:** Her mother's locket, carrying Lena's portrait — Jax has been wearing her face for eleven years without knowing whose face it was. She sees it. He sees that she sees something. The full weight waits for Ch 28.
- **Lena/Aria-7 relationship:** They know each other, or know of each other — their dynamic at the meeting suggests prior contact. Aria-7 and Lena are the team's most natural pairing; this is established quietly here.
- **Aria-7 literal-mindedness:** First team introduction of this trait. It will be a recurring source of both humor and genuine missed connection throughout Acts 2-3.
- **Jax's peripheral awareness of Lena:** Not named, not interpreted. Present as texture. The reader will understand it better after Ch 28 and Ch 38.

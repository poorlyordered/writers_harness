## Chapter Type

First Trial / Flaw Visible. The first real trial of Jax's latent Daybringer abilities — with Nalani's guidance, Aria-7's analytical support, and no preparation adequate for what he finds. The new world's most demanding elements: he is not just a miner anymore. What he can do feels foreign in his body. First success is partial. His flaw appears in its clearest form: he faces it alone, without asking for the help standing next to him. The team gets its first look at what Jax is carrying that isn't in his mining record.

## Story Circle Beats

**"Return" phase — new world demands.** The trial sequence (Ch 14-16) is the chapter's external spine, but its internal purpose is to make Jax's flaw structural and visible: he will not ask for help, even with people who have the exact capabilities his situation requires. Nalani can guide him; Aria-7 can analyze him; the team can support him — and he will receive their support only after he has exhausted every solitary option. This is not stubbornness; it is the wound in action. He has survived alone for eleven years. He does not know how to survive otherwise.

## POV Character

Jax Tarkin. The trial should be written with physical specificity — what the ability feels like from the inside, not what it looks like from the outside. Foreign, resistant, like operating equipment through unfamiliar controls. His internal monologue during the trial is practical and problem-solving; the emotion is in what he doesn't say when it goes wrong.

## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — Setup**
Nalani's guidance has been arriving through comms — the team is moving toward Sanctuary Valley but has not arrived; she is working with them remotely, which is a different register than the face-to-face of Ch 7-8 and introduces a slight friction of distance. She has identified that what happened in Ch 10's attack — the shard flaring, the uncontrolled burst — was not random. It was a first involuntary expression of the Daybringer abilities. They need to be trained, not because the prophecy requires it but because an uncontrolled expression in a crowd could hurt people.

Aria-7 has prepared an analytical framework for the trial — she has access to Varr records of how Daybringer abilities were historically developed and has generated a training protocol. She presents it to Jax with careful precision. He looks at the protocol. He does not say thank you. He asks one sharp, practical question about the physical parameters. She answers it. He nods.

**Scene 2 — The Trial**
The trial itself. The specific ability being trained is the controlled version of what happened in Ch 10 — a focused directional force rather than an omnidirectional burst. The shard is the interface; the ability moves through him and through it; the question is whether he can shape it rather than just survive it.

He cannot, at first. The ability is real — it comes when he reaches for it — but it comes with none of the precision the trial requires. The shard responds, but to his intention and his emotion together rather than to intention alone, and his emotion is not as disciplined as he would need it to be. He has two partial successes in the course of the trial: one where the output is approximately correct but far too large, one where the output is sized correctly but aimed wrong.

He does not ask for help during the trial. Nalani, remotely, offers guidance at one point — a specific suggestion about the quality of attention required, something she knows from Varr records about how the ability is accessed. He listens. He applies it imperfectly. The second partial success comes after the suggestion, which is the closest thing he has to a win.

Aria-7 is observing and recording. At the end of the trial she provides a precise summary of what worked, what didn't, and the specific parameters where his output was accurate. She does this with complete, careful precision. He receives it without deflecting. This is, for him, a significant act of openness.

**Scene 3 — After the Trial**
The team has been present — not all of them, but some — and their reaction to what they witnessed is the chapter's closing note. They saw something that was not in his mining record. The scale of what is riding on this mission is now legible to them in a way that the narrative of *stopping Drayton* does not fully capture.

Jax does not engage with their reaction directly. He is cleaning up from the trial — there is something to clean up from; the partial successes left evidence — and he is doing it alone, with the deliberateness of someone putting the work away for the night. Mira watches him from a distance and says nothing. This is her version of respect.

He goes over what Aria-7 said in his head. The second partial success: what was different about that moment. He does not write it in the journal. He sits with it.

*First success is partial. His flaw appears: he faces it alone, without asking for the help standing next to him.*

## Subplot Threads

- **Daybringer ability:** Physical, real, partially accessible, not controllable on demand. The shard is the interface. The emotion-intention problem will matter in the later trials.
- **Jax's flaw visible:** He will not ask for help. He takes the one piece of guidance Nalani offers because it comes with authority he has accepted; he does not ask for more. Aria-7's post-trial analysis is the closest he comes to collaboration.
- **Aria-7's analytical support:** She is providing something essential and she knows it. The gap between her data-delivery and the human acknowledgment she doesn't quite know how to receive is introduced here quietly.
- **Team as witness:** They see something about Jax that changes the texture of their understanding. This will matter when the trials go worse.

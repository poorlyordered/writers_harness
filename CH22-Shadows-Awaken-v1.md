## Chapter Type

Secondary Arc Beat / Misbelief Crack (Aria-7). The first event that breaks Aria-7's misbelief — something happens that her logic cannot account for, and she names this honestly rather than rationalizing it away. Her gap between analysis and understanding becomes a story point rather than background texture. The chapter establishes her arc as its own weight inside the ensemble.

## Story Circle Beats

**"Return" phase — secondary character fracture.** Aria-7's arc runs parallel to Jax's: both are operating from a misbelief that will be cracked and eventually resolved by the end of the book. Her misbelief is that emotional responses are inefficiencies rather than data. Ch 22 is the first event that makes this position untenable: something happens that she cannot process through logic, and her honest naming of this gap is the chapter's most important moment. She does not resolve it here. She acknowledges it. This is, for Aria-7, the equivalent of Jax's *I'll consider it* in Ch 8.

## POV Character

Jax Tarkin remains the primary POV, but Aria-7 should be the scene's center of gravity. His observation of her — the way he registers the fracture without quite knowing what to do with it — is the scene's frame. He has not been watching for this; he sees it when it happens.

## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — The Event**
Something happens to or around a team member that produces a significant emotional response — not in Aria-7, but visible to her, and carrying an emotional content that her analytical framework cannot fully account for. Options: Mira's armor fails in a way that exposes something she has been protecting; Toby does something that does not parse as rational self-interest; Lena's guilt manifests in a moment of behavior that Aria-7 cannot file under any category she has. The event is real and specific; the specific event should be chosen for the maximum contrast with Aria-7's processing capacity.

What Aria-7 experiences: she observes the event, generates a prediction about how the person should respond, and the person does not respond according to the prediction. Not because they are being irrational — because the prediction was built on a model that does not include the actual variable driving the behavior. The missing variable is emotional. She knows the variable is missing; she does not know how to acquire it.

**Scene 2 — The Naming**
Aria-7 says something — to Jax, or to Lena, or to the empty air in the specific way she processes aloud when she is working through something she cannot resolve internally. She says something like: *I have encountered a situation my current models cannot account for. The gap is not in the data. It is in my capacity to process a class of data that I do not yet have a category for.* She does not use the word "emotion." She gets very close to it. She stops before she arrives.

Jax receives this without knowing exactly what to do with it. He says something short and not particularly illuminating. He means it. It is, for her, more useful than he understands.

**Scene 3 — After**
Aria-7 continues her work — recording, analyzing, providing the support she is equipped to provide. But she files the gap separately from the rest of her analysis. She has named it; she will return to it. The arc has begun.

Something small changes in how she observes the team in the chapters that follow. The reader should notice; Jax does not.

*Her misbelief has its first crack. She acknowledged the gap rather than rationalizing it away. This is the Aria-7 version of "I'll consider it."*

## Subplot Threads

- **Aria-7's arc established:** The chapter is the formal opening of her secondary arc. She is not background — she is a character with her own question and her own progression toward answering it.
- **The gap she names:** Emotional data as a category she lacks access to. She can observe behavior; she cannot model motivation when motivation is rooted in something she doesn't have. This will matter when the team's emotional architecture is most under stress.
- **Jax's response:** Short, practical, not profound. And yet it is what she needs — not a lecture on emotion but evidence that someone received what she said without dismissing it. He does not know he did this. The reader does.
- **The team as emotional data:** Every team member's behavior from this point onward is, for Aria-7, also a data collection effort. This changes the texture of her presence in subsequent chapters.

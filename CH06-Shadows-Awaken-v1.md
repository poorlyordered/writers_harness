## Chapter Type

Inciting Incident / Decision Point. The dream erupts past refusal: a woman's face, almost held, then gone into nothing. The shard glows visibly for the first time — external, undeniable, impossible to rationalize away. Nalani's second apparition arrives briefer and more urgent than the first, saying only enough to make pretending impossible. This is the chapter where Jax decides. He does not decide joyfully or easily or with anything resembling acceptance — but he decides. He will go to Sanctuary Valley.

## Story Circle Beats

**"Find" phase** — the call becomes impossible to refuse because the cost of refusal is now legible. The dream fragment is not just strange anymore — it is the shape of something he has lost, and the nothing is not just an abstract wound but the specific absence of a specific person he cannot name. The shard's visible glow is the physical world confirming that his internal experience is real and connected to something external. Nalani's second apparition is the final push: she does not argue with his refusal; she simply makes the refusal untenable.

## POV Character

Jax Tarkin. The dream sequence should be written in close third with the specific texture of his internal voice — sensory, concrete, registered without interpretation. When the woman's face appears and is lost, the reader should feel the loss as loss, not as narrative event. His decision at the end of the chapter is not a breakthrough; it is a man who has run out of other options.

## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — The Dream Erupts**
Night. The dream comes — but this time it does not stop at the flash and the pain and the nothing. It goes further: the room he almost glimpsed in Ch 2, more solid now, and in the room — movement, presence, and then a face. A woman's face, specific enough to be a person rather than a blur, not-held for one terrible fraction of a second before the nothing takes it. He wakes with his hands gripping the edge of his bunk as if he could hold onto what just escaped him.

He lies still. He does not get up. He does not open the tin box. He lies still and waits for the loss to recede to a manageable distance.

The feeling does not recede. It is not grief, exactly — you cannot grieve what you never consciously had. It is the specific ache of something taken before you knew you were holding it.

*Memory thread: first real fragment — a woman's face. He doesn't know it's Lena.*

**Scene 2 — The Shard Glows**
Morning. He goes through the pre-shift routine and when he opens the tin box the shard is glowing. Visibly, unmistakably — not a hum, not a pulse, but light coming from inside the mineral. Cold light, steady, not fire. He stares at it for a long moment. He picks it up; the glow intensifies slightly in his hand. He sets it down; it continues, slower, like a held breath.

He closes the tin. He puts the tin back on the shelf. He stands in his quarters for a while without deciding anything.

He takes the tin off the shelf, opens it, takes out the shard. He puts the shard in his pocket. He goes to work with it glowing faintly through the fabric.

**Scene 3 — Nalani's Second Appearance**
During shift — the timing is inconvenient and she chooses it deliberately, Jax will understand later. The shard pulse-brightens in his pocket. Nalani's projection arrives: briefer than the first, the urgency clear without being performed. She does not argue with his refusal. She does not repeat the argument from Ch 4.

She says, in the direct way of someone who has watched him carefully and knows what will land: *Drayton's people are in the region. The Valley is not safe forever and neither is this colony.* She names something specific — not a prediction, but a fact she knows from sources she doesn't explain — that makes the timeline concrete. She says: *You've been looking for eleven years. Sanctuary Valley has what you're looking for. Come.*

She fades.

The shard dims to its new resting state — brighter than it was before Ch 4, a permanent upward shift.

**Scene 4 — The Decision**
He finishes the shift. He returns to his quarters. He sits with the decision the way he sits with difficult geological assessments — not emotional, working through the available data, building toward the option that makes the most sense under the conditions. The conditions are: he has been looking for Stellarium for eleven years and the lead he has been dismissing as supernatural has specific knowledge and a specific location and a clear statement of timeline pressure. The conditions are: the dream fragment. The conditions are: he has work to do, and the work he has been doing here is not the work.

He opens his journal. He writes: *I'm going to Sanctuary Valley.*

He closes the journal. He starts thinking about the crossing — what he knows about the terrain, what he'll need, how long. His mind has already moved past the decision to the logistics. This is how Jax makes hard choices: he commits, then plans, in that order, because standing still in the commitment is not something he does.

*The decision is made. Not acceptance — logistics. The misbelief remains: he is going because the data points that way, not because he was called.*

## Subplot Threads

- **Memory thread:** The woman's face in the dream — present for a moment, then gone. This is the first real fragment. It will not resolve until Ch 38.
- **Shard:** Reaches new permanent state — visibly responsive, carried on his person. The signal from Sanctuary Valley is now strong enough that the shard is no longer dormant.
- **Nalani's knowledge of Drayton:** She mentions the timeline pressure without explaining her intelligence network. This is the first suggestion that the Dissenter community has operational knowledge of Drayton's movements.
- **Decision register:** He decides in the register of logistics, not destiny. This is the misbelief doing what misbeliefs do: he will tell himself he chose this because the data supported it.

# Craft Reference: Deep Third-Person POV
*Source: Tiffany Yates Martin via Jane Friedman*

Deep third-person — where the narrator's voice is the character's voice — offers the intimacy of first-person with more flexibility. The risk: distance (action tags like "Jane felt") and ambiguity (pronoun confusion when observing other characters).

---

## Six Techniques

**1. Change pronouns to articles.**
Replace *his/her* with *the/this/that/a/an* to eliminate pronoun ambiguity.
> "Oliver was standing at the door to the bedroom." (not "his bedroom")

**2. Add descriptions.**
Key details enhance characterization and clarify whose perception we're in.
> "Olive handed her the silver-sequined wallet." (the detail belongs to Pauline's awareness)

**3. Add physical sensations.**
If only the POV character can feel it, the POV is unambiguous.
> "Olive squeezed Pauline's fingers so hard they hurt."

**4. Drop in backstory, character knowledge, or voice.**
If only the POV character knows it or would articulate it that way, the POV is clear.
> "Olive was breathing fast with her eyes squeezed shut, like that cousin from Boise who was afraid of the dark."

**5. Use character assessments.**
Words like *probably*, *must be*, *weirdly* show who is observing. Use the gap deliberately.
> "Olive was probably bored." (vs. "Olive was bored" — which reads as Olive's POV)

**6. Bury tags in the middle; tie them to action.**
If a tag is necessary, bury it mid-sentence or make it the result of the POV character's action.
> "Paul looked over at Oliver and wished he would stop hunching his shoulders..." (tag becomes result of action)

**Parting rule:** If you must choose between distance and ambiguity, take distance. But first look for a technique that eliminates both.

## Application Notes — *Shadows Awaken*

**POV character:** Jax Tarkin throughout all 40 chapters. Deep third, close. His register: procedural, tactile, guarded. Emotion through action, not declaration.

**Standing POV anchors:**
- The shard as felt object (Technique #3) — every scene where the shard is active, we're unambiguously inside Jax
- Miner's-eye assessments of people and terrain (Technique #4/5) — "the way he read terrain" is his established mode
- Short declarative internal assessments — lean, laconic, in his voice

**The Act 2A challenge (Chs 11–20):**
Jax goes from solitary POV to observing four characters in close quarters. Each has significant interiority the reader wants — Lena especially. The tools:

- **Technique #5 is the primary fix for Lena.** She has enormous interiority Jax can't access. Use the gap deliberately: *Lena went still for a moment. Probably wariness.* The "probably" keeps us in Jax's POV while signaling his read is incomplete. The reader knows more than he does. That gap is the B story engine.
- **Each team member gets one recurring Jax assessment** that grounds their presence in his POV — through his miner's lens, not their own interiority.
- **Technique #3** anchors every shard moment. One-directional sensation = unambiguous POV.

**The Act 2B pressure (Chs 21–30):**
Jax's misbelief is under sustained attack. The close third-person means the reader starts seeing things Jax doesn't want to name. His POV becomes increasingly unreliable about its own motivations — hold that without breaking the technique. The prose carries what he notices AND what he refuses to conclude.

**The Act 3 shift (Chs 31–40):**
After Ch 37's resolution, the POV should feel subtly different — same technique, different man. The armor is gone. We're inside someone who has stopped fighting what he is. The procedural register is still there, but it's no longer doing the work of keeping things out.

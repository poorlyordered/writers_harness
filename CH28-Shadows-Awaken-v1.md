## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — She Chooses the Moment**
She finds him. Not by accident — she has made a choice about timing, and the timing is deliberate: she has decided that the next time she has access to him alone, before the mission moves to a point where the truth would find a different way out, she is going to tell him. She has been making this choice since Ch 26. The chapter opens with her finding him.

He registers, when she sits down across from him or stands in the same space, that this is not a normal interaction. The quality of her attention is different — she is not protecting anything anymore. Or she is protecting everything by releasing it. He can feel the difference without naming it.

**Scene 2 — The Truth**
She tells him. The full truth. Not in order of importance but in the order it comes out, which is the order of cost: she starts with the accident, because the accident is the foundation of everything else. The lab. The experiment. The moment when something she didn't know she had manifested and destroyed something she could not restore. She names what the accident took from him. She names what she has been doing since — the years, the search, the finding him, the months of carrying this while standing next to him.

The locket. She tells him about the locket — whose it was, what it contained, how it came to be in his possession. *My mother's locket. She carried my face. It was lost in the accident. You have been carrying my face for eleven years without knowing whose face it was, and I have been looking at it on you and not saying anything.*

She stops. She does not ask for anything. She does not frame the truth as a request for forgiveness — she frames it as an obligation, something she owed him whether it cost her the mission or the team or whatever came next.

**Scene 3 — The Silence**
He does not respond immediately. He sits with what she has said in the way he sits with things that are too large for immediate response — not processing, not deciding, but in the specific suspension of someone who has just had both pillars of their self-understanding taken away simultaneously. He is hearing what she said. He is hearing the echo of Ch 13's *"the only thing that's ever felt like mine"* against what she just told him. He is hearing the locket's full weight landing on eleven years of carrying it without knowing why.

He does not say anything.

She waits. She does not fill the silence. She has said what she came to say. The rest is his.

The chapter ends without him answering. The scene is not resolved where it is spoken.

*The detonation. The locket's full weight arrives. Both pillars of his self-understanding collapse simultaneously — though the collapse won't complete until Ch 29.*

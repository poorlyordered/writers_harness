## Chapter Type

First Apparition / Initial Call. The first direct contact between Jax and Nalani via Stellarium projection. This is not a hallucination — Nalani is physically in Sanctuary Valley and the shard is the medium of transmission. The chapter's job is to make the apparition real before Jax can dismiss it: she knows things she has no logical right to know. He says no. She is not discouraged. The chapter closes with Jax drawn toward the Keeper Quarter — the want deepens even as the refusal holds, and the flaw becomes visible. He is not honest with himself about what he needs.

## Story Circle Beats

**"Search" phase** — Nalani's call reaches Jax across distance. The want deepens: the tin box, the locket, the dream, the shard — she knows all of it without being told. His refusal is his flaw in action: he cannot accept guidance he cannot verify with data, cannot trust what he cannot explain, cannot admit that the need underneath the want is not for Stellarium but for the past Stellarium might unlock. The pull toward the Keeper Quarter after she fades is his body answering a question his mind won't ask.

## POV Character

Jax Tarkin. The apparition scene is the chapter's center of gravity — write it so the reader cannot doubt that Nalani is real. Her knowledge of the tin box contents is the proof: Jax never told anyone. The shard's behavior during the apparition confirms the connection. His refusal should read as genuine, not theatrical — he means it when he says no.

## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — The Projection Arrives**
Not at night — during the day, in a quiet moment. Jax is doing something routine in his quarters, off-shift. The shard pulses — stronger than Ch 3, sustained rather than brief — and then Nalani is there. Not in the room in any material sense; a projection, a transmission through the shard, a real perception rather than a hallucination. She is small and unhurried in the way she appears: the calm of someone who has been waiting for this conversation for a long time and is not going to rush it now that it has arrived.

She looks like she belongs to a world older than DE. She speaks first. She is not asking permission. She knows his name and uses it without ceremony — *Jax* — and she begins.

**Scene 2 — She Knows**
The weight of the scene is in what she knows. She names the tin box. She describes the items inside — the locket, the shard, the note in his handwriting — without being told. She names the shard specifically, calls it something in a language he doesn't recognize that still lands with weight: it is not a random mineral fragment, it is a piece of something larger, and it has been with him because it is supposed to be with him. She speaks carefully about the locket — does not give full weight, does not explain who the locket once belonged to or what it signifies, but acknowledges that he has been carrying something whose meaning he cannot access, and that the meaning is real, and that the real meaning will matter.

She tells him, plainly and without drama, that he is needed somewhere. That Sanctuary Valley is real. That the prophecy she has spent her life in service to is not about a symbol — it is about a man, specific and present, and the man is him.

Jax says no. His exact words should land as genuine: not *I'll think about it*, not *maybe*, but no. He is a miner. He has work. He does not accept missions from apparitions who arrive through crystals and know things they shouldn't know — that is not evidence, that is a red flag.

She is not discouraged. She looks at him with the patience of someone who expected this exact response and finds it reasonable. She says something that does not argue with his refusal but sits next to it, the way certain truths sit next to denial: *You've already been looking. You've always been looking.* She fades. The shard settles.

**Scene 3 — Toward the Keeper Quarter**
Jax sits with the absence she left. He is not shaken — he processes the experience the way he processes everything: methodically, building toward an explanation that satisfies. He does not find one. The shard is quiet in the tin. The note in his handwriting: *You were looking for something. Keep looking.*

He goes outside. It is not an intention — it is what his body does when his mind has reached the limit of what it can work through sitting still. He walks. His feet take him in the direction of the Keeper Quarter.

He doesn't go in. He stops at the edge of it — the small cluster of buildings, the plantings, the different quality of the light that falls there, as if the buildings were arranged to hold it rather than deflect it. One of the residents is outside. Not the one who made eye contact before — someone older, sitting in the quiet way of someone who has been waiting for a specific person to arrive at their threshold for a very long time.

The resident does not speak first. Jax doesn't either. After a moment, Jax turns and walks back to his quarters.

*Want deepens. Flaw visible. He will not ask the question that is standing right in front of him.*

## Subplot Threads

- **Nalani introduction:** Her first appearance should establish the quality of her presence before her content — the patience, the certainty in this form, the sense that she is operating from a timeline much longer than Jax's. She is not pleading. She is presenting.
- **Locket thread:** She speaks about it carefully — not explaining, but acknowledging. The reader now knows the locket has specific significance Jax cannot access. It will land fully in Ch 12.
- **Keeper Quarter:** The resident who waits. Jax reaches the threshold and turns back. This is his flaw as geography: the answer is there; he won't cross.
- **Misbelief active:** "My choices are mine. I'm not a prophecy." He means this completely. The reader should be able to feel the cost of that certainty.

## Scene 3 -- Toward the Keeper Quarter

He sat down at his desk.

The journal was open to the page he'd been writing on. The account of the mine apparition: two days ago, northeastern shaft, beyond the boundary marker. He picked up his pen and looked at it and set it back down. He was not going to write about this yet. He did not have the category for it and writing before the category arrived produced records that were less useful than the absence of records.

He opened the tin box instead.

The locket. Oval, silver, the surface worn where his thumb always rested. She had said he would open it when the time came. He turned it in his fingers. He thought about opening it now -- not because it was the right time but because someone had told him there was a right time, and he had a standing objection to being managed, even by true things. His thumb found the latch.

He set it back.

Not yet. Not because she had told him not to, but because he wasn't ready, and being not ready was different from being told to wait. He was allowed to not be ready. He put the locket back, looked at the note, read it the way he had read it ten thousand times without the reading changing anything.

You were looking for something. Keep looking.

He closed the tin. He sat for a few more minutes. Then he went outside, because sitting had run its course and outside was what came next when sitting ran its course.

The colony in the late afternoon had the amber quality it always had at this hour, everything caught in the mineral light, the mine head structures and the processing towers and the residential blocks all rendered in the same specific gold that belonged to this world and no other. He walked without directing his feet, which was how he walked when he had more to process than his attention could hold. His feet knew DE the way they knew the survey corridors: without consultation.

He stopped at the edge of the Keeper Quarter.

He had not decided to come here. He was here. The cluster of older buildings, the plantings, the quality of the light that collected between the structures rather than passing through. A different resident than the woman with the sealant tool -- someone older, seated on the low step outside the nearest building, doing nothing in particular. Not waiting in the anxious sense. Sitting the way a rock sat in a current: present without effort.

The resident looked up at him. Said nothing.

Jax said nothing.

The Keeper Quarter was ten meters in front of him. Whatever it held was ten meters in front of him. The woman in the mine and the woman doing repair work on this door frame had looked at him the same way, and an apparition in his quarters had known the contents of his tin box and named the shard in a language that predated his sector's history. Ten meters.

He stood there for the length of time it took to decide and not decide and decide again.

Then he turned and walked back to his quarters.

He had work to do in the morning. The survey logs needed annotation and the scanner had two readings he was watching and the annotation for today's shift was still in his head rather than on the record where it belonged. He had work to do.

He did not know if the looking the note meant was the looking he had been doing. He had always assumed they were the same thing. He was less certain of that now.

# Chapter 3 -- Link to Antagonist

## Scene 1 -- News Moves Through the Colony

The news came through Okafor's the way most things came through Okafor's: without announcement, carried in pieces by three different people who each had a fraction of it, assembled by whoever was paying attention when the pieces arrived.

Jax was on his second cup of canteen-grade coffee, standing at the counter near the door the way he always stood, when the first piece came in. A convoy worker off an overnight corridor run -- young, still in his transit gear, the specific look of someone who had been awake for too many hours and was processing what he'd heard during them. He settled at the table behind Jax and started talking to the person already there: a mining crew three sectors over hadn't reported. Standard operation, no outstanding safety flags. Two comms cycles of silence.

Jax didn't turn around. He listened.

The man at the table asked the obvious questions. The convoy worker answered them the way someone answered questions they'd already been over several times in their head: briefly, with the impatience of a person whose own processing had moved past the obvious into something they hadn't resolved yet. Last recorded contact was routine. No distress signal. Equipment had pinged at its six-hour interval and then stopped pinging. The convoy worker had heard this from the transit hub dispatcher, who had heard it from the claim operator's emergency contact, which was now two removes from the source and losing resolution at each step.

The second piece came two hours later, in the equipment bay at the locker station. The man running the adjacent bank was relaying something he'd heard from the night shift foreman about the same situation, with a different detail attached: someone had pulled the last comms log from the missing crew's equipment. Nothing unusual in it. A scan result. A minor equipment flag, the kind that showed up three times a shift and got noted and ignored. A weather visibility note. And then the log ended, cleanly, the way a log ended when the equipment stopped rather than when the operator signed off.

The man at the adjacent locker bank said the word "fast" twice. The first time as description. The second time because the first time hadn't contained everything he meant by it.

Jax checked his scanner charge and said nothing.

The third piece was at the shift assignment queue, and this piece was different from the first two because it came with witnesses rather than reports. Two workers had transferred in overnight from a smaller extraction operation -- independent outfit, positioned one sector closer to the missing crew than DE was. They were quiet. Not the quiet of people who had nothing to say; the specific quiet of people who had decided what they were and weren't going to say and were holding the line on it. One of them, a woman with the look of someone who had been in extraction work for a long time and had seen things go wrong before, said when pressed by the person in front of her in line: it had not looked like an accident. It had looked like someone who knew what they were doing and had done it before and had not left anything behind them that explained what had happened.

What it had looked like beyond that she didn't say. Nobody pressed her for it. The line moved forward.

Jax took his assignment and went to work.

He had three pieces now. He ran through them the way he ran through survey data: not rushing toward a conclusion, building the picture from what was actually in it. A crew gone silent with no warning, no distress call, no mechanical explanation in the final log. Equipment that pinged on schedule and stopped pinging. A competitor's operation shut down -- not abandoned, not failed: shut down -- in a way that left one of its workers unable to describe what she had seen without deciding not to describe it. Workers arriving at DE not because DE was better but because whatever had happened made staying the less tolerable option.

Someone was moving through the region. Moving with purpose and in a way that knew what it was doing and had resources behind it and did not appear to be concerned about what it left behind. The scale of that was legible. The shape was legible.

What he didn't have yet was the reason underneath it: what someone would want badly enough from this region to work it this way.

He filed the absence of an answer and went into the mine.

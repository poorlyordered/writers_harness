# Chapter 15

## Scene 1 — Into the Trial

The second trial's target was smaller and the parameter was different in a way that felt like a different problem entirely.

Aria-7 had arranged a series of material samples along the far end of the cleared deck — the same section after Quill's repair work had made it functional again, a fact Jax was aware of in the background of everything. Three samples in three distinct configurations: a plate of dense composite on the left, the familiar gray cylinder at center, and to the right a length of something with a crystalline internal structure that caught the light wrong. The task this time was not to move or impact but to affect the crystalline sample's internal structure at range without disturbing the plates on either side. A shaped application rather than a directed one.

Nalani's voice came through with its quarter-second lag. "What Aria-7 has described as the difference between yesterday's trial and today's is accurate," she said. "The first form — projection — is the cruder form. The ability as the Varr documented it was capable of much finer resolution. What we're looking for today is the beginning of that resolution." A pause that felt deliberate. "You've been thinking about the emotion-intention problem since yesterday."

It wasn't a question.

"Yes," he said.

"Tell me your theory."

He had not intended to share it. He shared it anyway, because withholding it would have been a form of keeping himself from useful correction. "The intention has to come first. Before the emotion. I've been reaching for the thing and letting the intention form during — but the emotion is already built up by the time I find what I'm reaching for. The intention should be set before I open the channel."

A silence from Nalani's end. Not quite agreement. "Try it and see what it does," she said. Which was not quite encouragement and not quite skepticism and was, he was beginning to understand, her consistent method: let the work answer the question.

He looked at the crystalline sample between the two plates. Three centimeters of clearance on each side. Touch one without touching the other.

Aria-7, from her position at the table's edge: "The diagnostic sensors will record the resonance pattern in each material independently. I will have precise data on whether the effect crossed into the adjacent samples."

"Good," he said. He meant it.

Quill was running a calibration check on the sensors from a position at the starboard side of the space — about three meters behind and to Jax's right, which put him in the periphery of Jax's awareness in the specific way Quill tended to arrange himself in any working environment. Not in the way. Present enough to be useful.

He set the intention first. Held it — specific, bounded, the crystalline structure and its internal geometry and nothing adjacent to it. He held that shape the way he held a survey line: precise, not approximate. Then he reached for the ability.

The shard warmed. He held the intention and let the warmth build behind it.

## Scene 2 — The Failure

He released it and immediately knew it was wrong.

Not after — immediately. The quality of the output was different from the moment it left him, a wrongness in the channel between intention and expression that he felt the way you feel a tool slip before you see the result. The intention shape held but something else was off — direction, or angle, or something he didn't have a name for yet — and the output didn't go where the intention was pointing.

It went toward Quill.

Not directly. It spread slightly as it traveled, the way the first trial's initial attempt had spread, and what hit the starboard bulkhead two feet to Quill's left was enough to buckle the panel and throw heat into the space. Quill was already moving before the impact — one frame of him in motion, the fast and automatic movement of someone who had been in enough situations to develop a threat response that operated before conscious thought — and he cleared the hot zone with about eight inches of clearance. The buckled panel groaned. The sensor cluster Quill had been calibrating took the edge of the effect and went dark.

Jax stopped.

He went very still in the way of someone who has arrived at the result of a calculation and finds the result unacceptable. The shard had gone cold. The hollow in his chest was larger than yesterday's — deeper, the absence more present. He stood in it.

No one moved for a moment.

Then Mira was at the bulkhead, her hands on the buckled panel, running a fast structural assessment with the same quiet efficiency she brought to every problem: primary, secondary, what needs immediate attention versus what can wait. "Panel's deformed but not breached. Compartment integrity holds."

"Good," Toby said from the doorway, locating a foothold on solid ground and holding it. "That's good."

Quill was standing in the cleared space, looking at where he'd been a moment ago. He looked at the buckled panel. He looked at Jax. His expression was the specific expression of someone completing a rapid recalibration and choosing, deliberately, not to share the intermediate steps.

"So," he said. "That was the B attempt."

Nobody laughed. The line had aimed for the right register and landed somewhere adjacent to where the wound actually was, which was close enough that the room stayed quiet around it.

Aria-7: "The output deviated forty-four degrees from the intended vector. Preliminary resonance data indicates the crystalline sample was unaffected." She said this with her characteristic completeness, which meant she had also registered that Quill had cleared a burst with eight inches of clearance and was apparently unhurt. She registered all of it with the same even delivery.

Jax said nothing. He looked at the buckled panel. Then at the space where Quill had been standing. Then at his hands.

The calculation was simple: he had no reliable control over where it went. Two trials and the directional problem was not resolved, and today the directional problem had expressed itself in the direction of a person on his team. He had not aimed at Quill. He had also not been able to prevent the output from going toward Quill. The distance between those two facts was not as large as it needed to be.

"We're done for today," he said.

No one argued. Quill said, "I'll start on the sensor cluster," which was the most useful thing anyone could have said and was characteristic of him.

## Scene 3 — Still Here

He found what passed for alone on a ship this size — the forward section, outside the hours when Mira held her position there. The small window that looked at the same spread of transit space as every other window on the route, different only in being the one nobody was currently standing at.

He sat with it.

The self-doubt arrived the way it always arrived in him — not as a voice but as a weight. Not the panicked kind. The heavy kind, the kind that settles in and makes itself at home and doesn't require anything from you except that you acknowledge it's there.

Maybe Nalani was wrong.

Not wrong about the shard. Not wrong about the bloodline or the lore or the fever or the Varr records. Those were what they were and he had been carrying the evidence of them for eleven years without knowing what any of it was called. Maybe all of that was real and she was still wrong about him specifically — wrong about what the person who carried it needed to be capable of to do anything useful with it.

Eleven years in the same rock, doing the same work, and he had been good at it. Precision and force were tools he understood in his hands and in his body. He had never, in eleven years of difficult work in difficult conditions, released force in a direction he didn't intend and had it go toward a person instead.

The things the ability required of him were real. He understood the theory. He could describe the emotion-intention problem to Nalani with enough clarity that she'd told him to try it and see. And he had set the intention first, the way he'd theorized, and the output had still gone forty-four degrees off-axis and Quill had cleared it by eight inches.

*If this was always going to be mine, why doesn't it feel like mine?*

The thought arrived from somewhere he hadn't expected it to arrive from and landed before he could decide whether to let it. He looked at it for a moment. Honest — exactly as honest as the question was, and the question was very honest. He put it away without answering it, in the specific way of a man who has learned that some honest questions need to wait for more data before they can be answered usefully.

He got out the journal. The entry he wrote was:

*Trial 2. Almost hurt someone. Still here.*

He put the journal away.

He didn't quit. He didn't decide not to quit in some resolving internal speech — he simply sat with the weight and the real possibility that he should, and then the possibility became the past, and he was still there, and the next thing was the next thing. He would attempt a third trial. The directional problem was the problem. He would solve it or he would find out what solving it required. He had no other mode of operation.

The shard was warming slowly against his sternum. He pressed the back of his hand to his chest for a moment and felt the familiar exchange — his heat and its restore, the reciprocal thing that had been happening for eleven years without a name. His. Whoever he was. Whatever the ability required him to be.

From somewhere in the middle of the ship came Quill's voice explaining the sensor cluster's failure mode to Toby with the focused interest of someone who had found a new problem to solve and had no apparent memory of nearly being the origin point of it. Toby's reply was warm and redirecting in the way Toby's replies often were.

He let it settle around him.

Tomorrow. Third trial.

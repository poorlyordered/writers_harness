## Timeline

Absolute dating is unavailable for the earliest eras. The timeline below uses narrative reference points rather than calendar years, moving from the deep past toward the story's present.

**Era 1 — The Caeli Architects (Deep Past, Pre-Varr)**
The Caeli Architects were a race whose understanding of cosmic engineering exceeded anything the current era can reconstruct or fully comprehend. They built Stellarium — not as a mineral or a resource, but as an interface: a construct designed to reach across time and bridge civilizations toward a specific threshold. They seeded the Varr civilization, possibly deliberately. They left behind vast celestial constructs: artificial planetary remnants, megastructures, and the Stellarium Graves — the cores of ancient dead stars where the interface material can be physically accessed. They did not disappear. They withdrew. The distinction matters.

**Era 2 — The Varr Civilization (Ancient Past)**
The Varr rose into the gap the Caeli Architects left. Brilliant, expansive, and eventually fractured. They discovered Stellarium through the Graves and received the Daybringer prophecy through the interface. The prophecy described a single individual — blood of the Varr, marked by Stellarium, shaped by loss — who would stand at the threshold when the Caeli Architects returned. The Varr split over what this meant and what Stellarium should be used for. Two factions formed: those who treated the prophecy as a responsibility and Stellarium as a sacred instrument, and those who recognized Stellarium as power and pursued it accordingly. The fracture widened until it broke the civilization from within.

**Era 3 — The Varr Catastrophe (Ancient Past, End of Varr Civilization)**
The catastrophe faction triggered the apocalypse. The exact mechanism involved Stellarium misuse at scale — the precise nature of what they did is a Dissenter archive question, not yet public knowledge. The civilization collapsed. The Dissenter faction survived in hidden communities. Before the end, the mainstream Varr civilization — aware of the Dissenters' counter-influence — deliberately scattered the Dissenter communities across multiple planets to break their power and prevent them from operating as a unified body. This act of sabotage against the only people trying to preserve Varr knowledge is the foundational wound of the Dissenter identity: they were broken apart by the same civilization that destroyed itself, and then tasked with surviving to protect what remained.

**Era 4 — The Long Diaspora (Centuries of Hidden History)**
The Dissenter communities rebuilt in secret across multiple planets. Sanctuary Valley on Xanthius became one node in this network, eventually housing the most complete archive and producing the community's most capable leader. Different communities diverged in method and ethics across the generations of separation. Communication between nodes was careful and infrequent. The network was never fully reunified. What they shared was knowledge, memory, and the conviction that another Varr catastrophe — at galactic scale — was the outcome they existed to prevent.

**Era 5 — The Rift Awakening (Centuries Before Story)**
Multiple wormholes opened simultaneously across the galaxy without apparent cause. Previously isolated systems became connected overnight. Interstellar politics and trade realigned. The event was never explained to general satisfaction. Varr Dissenter records suggest it was not random — that the Rift Awakening was a precursor event tied to the Caeli Architects and the approaching threshold. The full meaning of this is a Book 3 revelation.

**Era 6 — The Modern Galaxy (Decades to Years Before Story)**
Post-Rift galactic civilization stabilized into the current power structure: Galactic Council, Stellar Dominion, Celestial Syndicate, independent systems. The Interstellar Navigation Guild leveraged its control of the QSN — built from Varr technology they do not fully understand — into outsized political influence. Stellarium became legend in mainstream culture: a black market curiosity, a political myth, a story treasure hunters tell. The Dissenters continued operating in secret. Cassius Drayton, Varr-descended from the catastrophe bloodline, began his twenty-year hunt for Sanctuary Valley.

**Era 7 — The Precipitating Event (Eleven Years Before Story)**
The Varr Dissenter network identified Jax Tarkin as the Daybringer and made a decision that defines Books 2 and 3: they orchestrated the lab accident on Xanthius that destroyed his memory. They chose Lena Vega as the instrument — her latent energy manipulation powers, triggered under stress during a Stellarium experiment. The accident was not random. The amnesia was intentional. Their reasoning was sound by their own logic, and it should not have been necessary. Jax woke on Desolation's Edge with no past, a battered tin box, and a pull toward Stellarium he could not explain.

**Current Moment — Story Present**
Eleven years after the accident. Nalani Kanoa remains in Sanctuary Valley, watching and waiting. Jax has been on Xanthius long enough that the Daybringer signal has been building — and Nalani has grown increasingly certain of who he is. She has not gone to him. She reaches him through apparitions she can project across distance, a Stellarium-tied ability that is hers alone. He will have to come to her. Cassius Drayton is closing in on the Valley's location. The signal is strengthening.

## Key Events

**The Building of Stellarium (Caeli Architects Era)**
The Caeli Architects constructed the Stellarium interface and placed access points within the cores of dying stars — the Stellarium Graves. These were not random locations. Dead stars are among the most stable long-term structures in the galaxy: the Graves were designed to persist until the threshold moment arrived. The interface is not merely a communication device. Its full function becomes clear only in Book 3.

**The Varr Discovery of Stellarium**
The Varr found the Graves and, in accessing them, received the Daybringer prophecy. This was not accidental contact — the interface was designed to be findable by a civilization at the right stage of development. The prophecy set in motion everything that followed: the fracture, the catastrophe, the Dissenter scattering, Jax's bloodline, the accident. Every event in the trilogy has its root in the moment the Varr first opened a Stellarium Grave.

**The Varr Catastrophe**
The catastrophe faction's misuse of Stellarium destroyed the Varr civilization from within. The Dissenters survived. The Caeli Architects' constructs survived. The Stellarium Graves survived. The prophecy survived. Everything built to last, lasted.

**The Dissenter Scattering**
Before the civilization fully collapsed, the mainstream Varr scattered the Dissenters deliberately — breaking them across planets to fracture their influence. The Dissenters rebuilt anyway. The network they constructed in diaspora is less unified, less consistent, and ultimately more resilient than a single centralized community would have been. The scattering backfired across centuries.

**The Rift Awakening**
The simultaneous opening of wormholes reshaped the galaxy's political map and created the trade infrastructure that the current era depends on. The QSN the Navigation Guild controls runs through corridors the Rift opened. The Dissenters' records flag it as non-random. Its connection to the approaching threshold is the final piece of a picture that Book 3 assembles.

**The Lab Accident (Eleven Years Ago)**
The Dissenter network's intervention. Lena Vega, a materials science researcher with latent energy manipulation powers, was working with Stellarium at a research facility on Xanthius. The Dissenters identified her as the instrument and allowed conditions to develop that would trigger her powers under stress. The accident injured Jax, wiped his memory completely, shut down the facility, and sent Lena running. It also separated him from everything that would have told him who he was — except the locket, which he kept without knowing what it was. The network chose this. They believed the Daybringer could not fulfill the prophecy with his prior identity intact. They may have been right. That does not make what they did to Lena acceptable.

## World-Shaping Conflicts

**The Varr Civil War / Catastrophe**
Not a conventional war — a fracture within a single civilization that escalated to apocalypse. The catastrophe faction's willingness to use Stellarium as a weapon, and the Dissenter faction's inability to stop them, ended the Varr. The lesson encoded in this conflict is the trilogy's foundational warning: Stellarium as power rather than instrument destroys the person who treats it that way, at any scale.

**The Dissenter Scattering**
A deliberate act of fragmentation by a dying civilization against its own conscience. The scattering created the network that now protects the galaxy — and the ethical divergence within that network that makes the Dissenters' methods a Book 2 crisis rather than a simple heroic backstory.

**The Rift Awakening and Its Aftermath**
Not a war, but a world-shaping event. The political realignments following the Rift created the current galactic power structure, including the Navigation Guild's leverage over the QSN. Control of FTL travel is the defining power variable in the current era; the Rift Awakening is why.

**Drayton's Twenty-Year Hunt**
Not a galactic conflict — a personal one. But Cassius Drayton's Stellarium fever, sustained over two decades, has left wreckage across the Xanthius region and beyond: mining operations shut down, crews that disappeared, competitors eliminated. He is not a lone obsessive; he has resources, reach, and the backing of interests who believe Stellarium is leverage. His hunt for Sanctuary Valley is the Book 1 external threat, and it is not separate from the deeper history. He is the catastrophe faction's bloodline, living out the same pattern his ancestors began.

## Current State

**Galactic governance:** The Galactic Council nominally manages interstellar relations. The Stellar Dominion and Celestial Syndicate are the dominant power factions, frequently in tension. Independent systems operate outside Council authority, including most frontier regions. The Navigation Guild controls FTL infrastructure with an independence that no governing body has successfully challenged.

**Stellarium in public consciousness:** A legend. Treasure hunters and black marketeers deal in artifacts claimed to be Varr-adjacent; most are fraudulent. A clandestine network of elite researchers and rogue archaeologists searches for real traces. The Keepers — Varr Dissenter communities, primarily Nalani's people — operate in the shadows of this ecosystem, watching who gets close and redirecting when possible.

**Sanctuary Valley:** Hidden on Xanthius, under Nalani Kanoa's unbroken stewardship. The most complete Dissenter archive in the network. Cassius Drayton has been closing in on its location for two decades. The Valley's security depends on obscurity; that obscurity is eroding. Nalani remains. The Valley is not left without its leader.

**The Dissenter network:** Multiple communities across multiple planets, diverged in method and ethics across generations of isolation. Not a monolith. Some communities share Nalani's philosophy; others do not. The network's full scope surfaces in Book 2. Its internal fractures become a crisis before Book 3.

**The signal:** Stellarium is responding to Jax's proximity in ways that have been escalating for months. The shard in his tin box hums. The dreams are sharpening. Nalani has felt it from Sanctuary Valley — and the feeling has been building long enough that she is certain now. She has not left to find him. She reaches toward him through projected apparitions — her way of preparing him without abandoning the archive. Drayton's instruments have registered anomalies near Desolation's Edge. The threshold is not imminent — but it is approaching. Everything the Dissenters have been protecting toward is beginning to converge.

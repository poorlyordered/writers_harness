## Chapter Type

Ordinary World Establishing / Protagonist Introduction. The reader's first full immersion in Jax Tarkin's eleven-year existence at Desolation's Edge. Everything in this chapter is calibrated to make the ordinary world feel earned: the grind of it, the competence of it, the haunted undercurrent of it. The misbelief is not stated — it is demonstrated. The shard hum at chapter's end is the first note of something the reader should feel before they understand it.

## Story Circle Beats

**"You" phase** — who Jax is and what his world costs him daily. The reader meets him inside the life, not approaching it. His capability is established through action. His wound is established through the dream and the tin box — present, examined, put away without answers. His care for others is established through the mine moment — he helps without comment, at cost to himself, which is the most important thing to know about him before anything else happens.

## POV Character

Jax Tarkin. POV is close third — his perception, his idiom, his laconic internal register. Observations are concrete and physical; he does not editorialize. What he notices reveals who he is. What he doesn't examine reveals his misbelief.

## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — The Dream**
Opens in the dream: a flash of blinding light, a spike of pain that doesn't register as pain so much as erasure, and then the nothing. Not darkness — nothing. Jax wakes. The dream is not new; it has been with him for eleven years, regular as a heartbeat, unchanged as stone. He doesn't lie in bed with it. He gets up.

The tin box is on the shelf where it always is. He opens it — the ritual of opening it, the items inside: the locket (sealed, never opened), the shard of Stellarium (inert in the tin, responsive to touch in a way he stopped trying to explain years ago), the folded note in his own handwriting that says only *You were looking for something. Keep looking.* He closes the tin. He has work to do.

**Scene 2 — The Mines**
Desolation's Edge in the early shift: the scale of it, the industry of it, the way a frontier mining colony looks when it is working rather than picturesque. Jax is competent here in a way that reads as earned rather than natural — he has learned everything he knows in eleven years and he is very good at it. He reads geological formations others miss. He works efficiently. He does not talk unless there is something to say.

Midway through the shift, a younger miner — not well-established, not named here, just present — gets into a dangerous situation. Not catastrophic; the kind of thing that happens in mines and sometimes costs people. Jax handles it. He doesn't explain himself or wait for thanks. The cost to him is minor but real — lost time, physical effort, a moment where his own work is exposed to risk. He absorbs it without comment and goes back to work. This is who he is, established through action.

**Scene 3 — End of Day / The Shard**
End of shift. The light on DE's surface is whatever color the planet's sun produces — describe it once, specifically, so the reader has a physical anchor for this world. Jax walks back to his quarters through the colony streets. The Keeper Quarter is at the edge of town — a small cluster of buildings, different in character from the mining infrastructure, with people who seem to belong to a different register of this world. He passes it without stopping, as he has for eleven years.

In his quarters, the tin box on the shelf. He doesn't open it again. He is cleaning his tools — a meditative activity, the kind of task that lets the mind settle — when the shard hums. Not loudly; barely perceptibly. But it is different from the usual nothing. He pauses. He looks at the tin. He finishes cleaning his tools.

*Misbelief active. Memory thread: the dream, the nothing.*

## Subplot Threads

- **Memory thread:** Dream — flash, pain, nothing. No fragment yet, no face. Just the nothing. The note in the tin box (*You were looking for something. Keep looking.*) is its own kind of wound — written in his hand, before memory, answering a question he cannot remember asking.
- **Shard:** Introduced here as a physical object that belongs to Jax's world. The hum at chapter's end is barely a signal — just enough for the reader to register that it is not entirely inert.
- **Keeper Quarter:** Passes it without stopping. Established as part of the landscape before it becomes significant.

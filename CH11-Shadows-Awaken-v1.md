## Chapter Type

First Plot Point / Team Shape Forming. Jax leaves Desolation's Edge. Not summoned — choosing. The distinction matters to him even as he refuses to examine why. Toby Blackwood has a ship and reasons of his own for leaving. Mira Stavros has no remaining contract and nowhere else to be. Quill Anders surfaces at the first transit stop beyond DE, through Keeper Quarter contacts who have been quietly expecting this moment. The team has a shape before it has a name. Jax does not explain himself. They do not require it.

## Story Circle Beats

**"Return" phase — entry point.** The chapter opens the second half of the story's first movement: Jax in motion, choosing forward, the misbelief immediately complicating the choice. The team formation is organic, not recruited — everyone present has their own reason to be moving in this direction, and those reasons are not fully disclosed. This is the chapter where Act 2 becomes possible, and the cost of that possibility is examined only briefly before Jax moves on to the next practical problem.

## POV Character

Jax Tarkin. His internal register in this chapter is logistics and forward momentum — he is processing the choice he made in Ch 10's aftermath by not stopping to process it. The misbelief operates as armor: *I'm choosing this. I chose this.* The reader can feel the effort that sentence requires.

## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — Leaving**
The morning after Ch 10's attack. DE in the aftermath: functional but changed, the colony recalibrating in the particular way that frontier settlements recalibrate when something has demonstrated that stability was always provisional. Jax is packed. He is not sentimental about leaving — there is no ceremony in it, because ceremony would imply he is acknowledging more than logistics. The tin box is with him. The shard is on his person.

He finds Toby at the landing pad. Toby's ship — not large, not new, but functional in the way of equipment that has been maintained by someone who depends on it — is being readied. Toby does not ask where they're going before saying he'll take them. He has his own reasons for leaving DE, which he does not state. The reasons are real; the silence about them is also real. Jax accepts this because he is not in a position to demand disclosure he is not offering.

Mira Stavros appears at the pad while they are still in the preliminary logistics. She has her kit. She does not ask for permission to come. She states, briefly, that she needs transit out of DE and is willing to work for it. No contract. No terms beyond that. Jax looks at her for a moment — reading her the way he reads terrain — and nods. She boards.

**Scene 2 — The First Transit Leg**
In transit. The ship's small crew space establishes the dynamic before any of them are ready to name it: three people who do not know each other well enough to have established patterns, sharing quarters with a level of physical closeness that either produces conflict or calibration. The calibration is beginning.

Toby's personality — easy, surface-level smooth, the kind of person who fills silence without demanding anything from it — is the chapter's social lubricant. He talks. Not about anything significant; about the ship, about routes, about the minor impracticalities of the transit. Mira says almost nothing. Jax says less than Toby and more than Mira. This is the team's natural register before it knows it's a team.

The shard, on Jax's person: quiet. Oriented. He is moving toward Sanctuary Valley; the signal is satisfied.

**Scene 3 — Quill Anders**
First transit stop beyond DE. A small hub — not a population center, just a place where routes intersect and people who need to not be found for a while come to be briefly unremarkable. Jax is handling logistics when someone finds him. Not a threatening approach — a comfortable, unhurried walk toward him from across the landing area, as if arriving at a meeting that was always scheduled.

Quill Anders. Mechanic. The Keeper Quarter contacts Jax never directly spoke to have been quietly expecting this moment for some time and have managed it accordingly. Quill knows about the mission in the abstract — he was briefed by the network, not by Jax — and approaches the situation with the studied unimpressedness of someone who has been told something significant and has decided to reserve judgment on whether it lives up to the description.

He introduces himself briefly. He mentions, as if this is incidental, that he is between jobs and has a particular set of skills with older-model Varr-adjacent technology that tends to be relevant in certain kinds of situations. He does not elaborate on what kind of situations. He does not ask to be invited. He assumes he is coming and behaves accordingly.

Toby thinks this is funny. Mira watches him the way she watches all new additions to any situation. Jax does not have the energy to argue with the structural logic of it.

The team has its shape. Six, eventually — four now.

*The first choice that feels genuinely his. And the misbelief immediately complicates it: Is he choosing, or following a trail?*

## Subplot Threads

- **Jax's misbelief under first pressure:** The choice to leave DE felt like his. The way Quill appeared — through a network he didn't activate, at a stop he chose for practical reasons — raises the question he will not ask aloud.
- **Toby's reasons:** He has them. They are not disclosed here. The reader should register their existence without knowing their nature. This thread will become a question in Ch 24-25.
- **Mira's adaptation:** She is already functioning as protective awareness for the group without having been asked. Her contract is gone; her instincts are not.
- **Keeper Quarter network:** Quill's arrival reveals that the network has been managing toward this moment at a level of operational detail Jax did not know about. The Dissenters are not passive.

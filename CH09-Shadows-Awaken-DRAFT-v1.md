# Chapter 9 -- Refusal of the Call

## Scene 1 -- Return to DE

The return crossing was shorter than it should have been.

He had no good explanation for this that he was willing to examine. The terrain was the same terrain, the distance was the same distance, but the approach that had taken him most of a day outbound resolved in under four hours on the return. The visual anomalies that had tracked him through the transition zone were absent. The weight in the air that had made certain directions harder than others was not present. The shard was quiet.

He filed this in the same category as everything else he was not going to examine, and walked back to DE.

DE resolved out of the plateau heat in the early afternoon: the mine heads first, then the smelter stacks, then the irregular sprawl of residential structures that had accreted without plan as the population had grown and sometimes shrunk and grown again. The specific smell of it -- machine oil, mineral dust, the particular undertone of a community that recycled its water more than it refreshed it -- arrived before the visual. He had always found this smell reassuring in the way familiar things were reassuring, not because they were good but because they were known.

He found it less reassuring than he had before. He noted this and kept walking.

His quarters were the same: the bunk, the shelf, the window that let in the amber light at the hours when it came in at the right angle. The journal on the table. The survey tools on their hooks.

The tin box on the shelf.

He took the shard from his jacket pocket and put it in the tin box and closed the lid. The motion was deliberate and without ceremony -- the same motion he used for equipment storage at end-of-shift, functional, complete. He set the box back on the shelf.

He unpacked the rest of the pack. He ate something from his stores. He lay on the bunk and stared at the ceiling until the space between thoughts grew large enough that sleep became unavoidable.

The dream came sometime in the early morning hours: the flash, the impact, the white. The face at the end of the white. He woke with the specificity of the face sharper than it had ever been in eleven years, and lay with it for three seconds before the specificity began to fade, and lay with the fading for three more, and got up and went to work because the shift was starting and he had work to do and this was the thing he had come back for.

The shard was quiet in its box. He left it there.

## Scene 2 -- Back to the Mines

The seam was where he had left it.

The geology was familiar in the way only eleven years of daily contact produced -- not memorized but inhabited, the way a person inhabits a space until their body makes the calculations automatically. He could read this formation without instruments. He had read it without instruments before the shard arrived and made him better at it, which was a fact he was currently not thinking about.

He worked. The shift had its rhythm: extraction, survey, transit, documentation, repeat. The other miners on his section knew him in the way people who worked the same seams knew each other -- a great deal of context and very little information. He answered questions with the required minimum and offered nothing they hadn't asked for. This was what they expected. This was why the dynamic worked.

The shard began to pulse at the end of the first hour.

Not loudly. Not in a way anyone nearby could have detected -- no heat, no visible light through the ore dust and work clothes. Just the quality he had spent eleven years categorizing as *unexplained -- possibly relevant* and one week learning had a more specific name. A directional pressure, oriented southwest. Toward the approach terrain. Toward the valley that was not on any map.

He adjusted his grip on the extraction tool to a position that dampened the contact point. He made this adjustment without pausing the work. He did not think about when he had learned what dampened the contact and what activated it, or what it meant that he knew.

The shift continued. The shard continued. He adjusted around it for eight hours with the specific internal stillness of a person who is managing something they have decided not to address.

That night the dream came differently.

Not just the face. The face had a room around it now -- the suggestion of walls he couldn't fully see, light from a source that wasn't resolved. And a voice: not audible, not words, but the quality of a voice at the very threshold of hearing, the sense of something being said that he was one step away from being able to understand. He woke with it already fading and lay very still, which did not help, and the fading completed as it always did.

He was more depleted the next morning than he had been the morning before. He had been more depleted each morning since the return. He attributed this to re-acclimation to DE's air quality, which was a plausible explanation and not the actual explanation.

He did not write any of this in the journal.

He went to work.

## Scene 3 -- Keeper Quarter Watch

Three days back, and the Keeper Quarter was watching in the way it always watched -- without watching.

He had been taking the evening route past it since his first year at DE, for the same reason he had always taken it: efficiency, the shortest path between the mine head and his quarters. He had never had occasion to think of this as a choice.

He was thinking about it now. The residents registered his return the way people registered an expected thing that had arrived slightly off schedule. Not with visible reaction -- with the quality of attention that did not need to announce itself because the attention had been in place for longer than the event it was waiting for.

A woman tended a window box on the ground floor of the second building, working with the unhurried method of someone who was outside because the evening was good and because being outside put her in a position to see the lane. A man sat on the steps of the fourth building with something in his hands, not looking up. Two younger residents moved between buildings at the pace of people who had nowhere to be quickly.

The man on the steps said, without looking up, without addressing anyone in particular: "He'll be back soon enough."

Jax's stride did not break. He kept his eyes on the lane ahead and kept walking.

Three questions formed and did not get asked.

The first was the one he already knew the answer to. Asking it would be confirmation of what the not-asking was currently allowing him to leave unconfirmed.

The second was about what they knew that he didn't, and asking it would acknowledge that there was something to know -- which acknowledged the network he had walked away from, which acknowledged that walking away from a network was a specific kind of departure, different from simply leaving.

The third was the worst. It was the question about timeline -- the one that lived in the gap between *if* and *when* and treated the distinction as already settled. He could see its shape from here. He did not walk toward it.

He had work to do. He knew this was exactly the kind of problem he was not going to solve by standing in a lane. He kept walking.

The three questions stayed with him for the rest of the evening and he did not examine that they were staying.

## Scene 4 -- The Journals

He opened the journal before he intended to.

He had sat down at the table meaning to review survey notes -- documentation from the week before the Valley trip that hadn't been formally logged. He opened the journal and found himself looking at the last entry.

*She said surviving alone isn't the same as living. She's right. I haven't decided what to do about it.*

He looked at this for a while.

He had not decided. He was aware that he had not decided, and that he had been not-deciding for four days now, and that the not-deciding had begun to occupy exactly the space that a decision would occupy -- the same recurrent priority, the same tendency to surface at the end of a physical shift when the work's demand no longer crowded everything else out.

The survey notes from before the Valley trip were in a different pocket. He had not processed them because processing them would mean logging them, and logging them would mean opening this journal, and opening this journal would mean encountering what he had written, and encountering what he had written would mean choosing whether to respond to it or close the journal again without adding to it.

He was closing the journal again without adding to it.

He set it on the table.

Across the room, the tin box on the shelf had a line of cold light running along the seam of its lid. Not bright -- the kind of light that required a dim room to see clearly, which this was. The same cold, blue-white steadiness the shard had found at the Shrine, unchanged by being enclosed, unchanged by being carried back across the transition zone, unchanged by his decision to box it and leave it and not look at it.

He looked at it from across the room.

He had work to do. He knew this was exactly the problem.

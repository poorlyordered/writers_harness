# Chapter 12

## Scene 1 — The Rendezvous

The location was the kind of place you could feel had been used before. Not lived in — used. The difference was in the quality of the surfaces: clean without being maintained, functional without being comfortable, with the specific orderliness of somewhere that regularly received people who needed to not be conspicuous and had organized itself around that need over a long time. A table. Four walls. Two exits. Light that worked.

Aria-7 was already there.

She was seated at the table when Jax's party came through the door — not waiting, precisely, but occupying the space with the quality of someone for whom waiting was simply another form of data collection. Her eyes went directly to Jax when he entered and stayed there. Not rude, exactly. Something else.

"Jax Tarkin," she said. Not a question. Her voice was smooth in the particular way of something that had been made rather than acquired — not unpleasant, but calibrated. "I'm Aria-7. I have analyzed the mission parameters. The primary objective requires accessing the Varr archive at Sanctuary Valley — the training continuation, the full historical record, and the operational base the mission needs. My capabilities include predictive modeling, deep familiarity with Varr technical systems, and pattern recognition at a scale your current team cannot replicate." A pause. "I am available."

Toby, standing just behind Jax's left shoulder, said quietly, "Available."

"Yes."

"Like — available available, or—"

"I mean that I have no conflicting obligations and have determined this mission to be the highest-priority use of my current operational time."

"Right," Toby said. "That's what I thought you meant." He did not sound entirely convinced. He sounded entertained, which was probably the more useful reaction.

Jax looked at Aria-7 the way he'd looked at Quill and Mira — reading for the structural truth. What he found was different from either of them. Not the peace of someone who had decided; something more like the certainty of someone who had calculated. The distinction was hard to name and would matter later or it wouldn't.

"How did you find this location," he said.

"Through a network I am not at liberty to identify by name. The same network that directed Quill Anders to your transit stop." She said this without emphasis, as if he had already processed it. "Is this a problem?"

It was and it wasn't, and the ratio was the same as it had been with Quill. He said, "Can you do what you said you can do."

"That statement invites verification, which I welcome. Yes."

Mira had taken up a position near the room's second door, cataloguing. Quill was looking at Aria-7 with the particular attention of someone who had encountered a new kind of variable and was deciding what category it belonged in. Toby had found a seat and looked prepared to be interested in whatever happened next.

"Fine," Jax said.

## Scene 2 — The Locket

Lena Vega arrived from a different direction.

The door she came through was not the one the rest of them had used — a detail that registered and held, the way details register when you've been paying attention to approach angles long enough that the habit has become automatic. She had traveled separately, on her own route, and arrived through a coordination that must have taken more managing than it appeared from the inside.

She was in her early thirties. Dark hair. Controlled movements in the specific way of someone who had spent years training herself not to be careless. She carried her kit with the efficiency of someone who had learned that carrying things costs you.

She came through the door and her gaze found Jax immediately — the kind of immediate that suggested the search had been running longer than this moment. Then something happened.

He wasn't sure what. She was looking at him, and then she was looking at something slightly below his face — at his chest, at his collar, at what hung there — and something changed in her expression that was not large and was not nothing. Not wariness. Something that arrived fully formed, that had to be immediately, carefully, put somewhere.

She put it somewhere.

Jax became aware of the locket in the specific way of a thing you've stopped noticing becoming visible again. It was at his collar, where it always was. He looked at her. She was looking at his face now, and her expression was controlled in the way of someone who had just spent a great deal of effort on that control and was not going to let the effort show.

She crossed the room. "Lena Vega. Materials science — Stellarium properties and behavior, primarily." Her voice was level. The kind of level that isn't the absence of something but the presence of something held very still. "The mission requires direct research experience. I have it."

She put out her hand.

He took it. Her grip was brief and specific, with the quality of someone who had prepared for exactly this moment and was executing it correctly. Her hand was steady.

He didn't know why *correctly* was the word that arrived.

"Where did you come from," he said.

"The same place as everyone else here." A pause. "Through the network. Through a longer route than most." She said this with a precision that was probably accuracy and might have been something else he didn't have an angle on yet.

She had brown eyes. They had the quality of someone monitoring their own reactions closely enough that the monitoring was almost visible — which he recognized because he had spent eleven years watching himself do the same thing. She stepped back from the handshake, turned slightly, and said, "Aria," with the ease of two people who had established their terms long enough ago to have stopped thinking about them.

"Lena," Aria-7 said, with the same ease and none of the warmth.

He filed all of it. The locket was where it always was. He did not touch it.

## Scene 3 — The Full Team

Six people in one room is a different thing than five.

The team had a weight to it now that it hadn't had in transit — not the weight of conflict but the weight of people who hadn't yet established the particular vocabulary of a group and were negotiating it without acknowledging that they were doing so. This was normal. This was how groups became teams: through small accumulations of unacknowledged negotiation.

Toby was talking about the route. Not because anyone had asked; because there was a map on the table and he had opinions and filling silence was what Toby did when silence otherwise seemed likely to accumulate into something heavier. His route analysis was sound. Quill was listening with the focused attention of someone parsing for technical relevance and filing the rest.

Aria-7 said, during a natural pause: "When you said 'it's not rocket science,' you were using a figure of speech. However, a portion of what you are describing does involve rocket propulsion mechanics."

A beat.

Toby looked at her. "I — yes. That's true."

"I mention it because accuracy in navigation discussion seems preferable to imprecision."

"It does," Toby agreed. He had the expression of a man recalibrating his assumptions in real time and finding the process genuinely enjoyable. "It really does."

Quill said, without particular inflection, "She's going to be very useful and also a source of significant personal suffering."

Aria-7 turned to him. "I don't understand that statement."

"I know," Quill said.

Mira watched this with the patient attention of someone building a model of how the room worked. She had not said more than six words since they'd arrived, which was about what Jax had expected.

Lena was at the far end of the table with a small device, reviewing something. She was present in the specific way of someone who occupied peripheral attention without doing anything to occupy it. He was aware of her. He did not examine what that meant.

He looked at the map. The route to Sanctuary Valley was not simple — it was designed not to be, which was a different problem than complicated-because-nobody-thought-it-through. Someone had thought it through extensively. Getting there meant moving through a particular sequence of systems, in a particular order, using stops the network maintained, in a way that would not surface to Drayton's intelligence operations. It was going to take time. Time Drayton was using right now.

He looked at the six people in the room and thought, in the flat way he thought about most things: *this is what we have. Make it work.*

"We leave at first transit window. Toby has the route." He looked at each of them once — the way you assess new equipment, checking for structural integrity. "Questions before then, ask. During — we work."

Nobody asked. Which was either confidence or the reasonable recognition that questions answered themselves once things started moving.

He folded the map. The locket hung where it always hung.

Somewhere across the table, Lena Vega was not looking at him.

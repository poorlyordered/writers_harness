# Chapter 29

## Beat 1 — The Exit

He did not respond.

He said nothing, and he did not move for a moment, and then he moved. Not dramatically. Not the exit of someone who has made a decision. The exit of someone whose body has identified that remaining in the space is not something it can do, and has begun moving before the rest of him has caught up.

He was at the archive's outer door. He did not know, precisely, how he had covered the distance. The door was in front of him. He went through it.

The Valley was dark and cool, the night sounds it made when it was settling: wind through the high growth, something small moving in the distance, the specific quality of open air after hours inside stone and record. He stood in it. He did not look back at the archive.

He walked to the ship. He had no reason to go there except that the ship was away from the archive and had a space below the equipment rack where the ceiling was low enough to require sitting, and sitting in a space that required it was not the same as choosing to sit. He needed things that required something of him. He needed the choice to be removed.

He went below the rack. He sat. The ship was quiet in the way of a ship not running: ambient systems, low hum, the occasional pressure sound from the hull responding to temperature differential. He knew all of these sounds. He had lived in them for months. They did not help.

He sat in the low-ceilinged space and he did not have a plan for what to do next, which was not a condition he was accustomed to. He was accustomed to having a plan. He was accustomed to being the person who was already three steps into the assessment while everything else was still arriving. He had built that capacity across eleven years and he had relied on it and it was not available to him now.

The archive was across the Valley. Lena was in the archive.

He thought this. He sat with it.

The hum of the ambient systems was constant. He was aware that he had left without saying anything to her and was aware that this was its own data, and was aware that he had nothing he could have said, and that the nothing was real and not a failure of language but a fact. There was nothing to say yet. He did not know if there would be.

He sat. He waited for the tools to come back online.

They did not.

## Beat 2 — The First Piece

She caused the accident.

He turned it over. He had a habit, with information that didn't fit, of holding it at different angles until the angle revealed a fit he had missed. He applied this. The information did not reveal a different angle. It had one angle and the angle was direct.

She had been seventeen. She had been a researcher on Stellarion Prime. She had designed a configuration from first principles across four years and had believed it was original, and it was not original, and when the experiment failed, others had not survived. That was eleven years ago. She had spent eleven years looking for the person the accident had marked. The person was him.

He had been in the room.

He did not have a memory of it. He had almost no memory of the years before the career: the extraction work had come early, had replaced whatever he had been before it, or he had always been this, or something between. He had a few clear things. A place. A voice he couldn't assign. The locket.

The locket.

He looked at it. He did not open it. He had never opened it in front of anyone and he had rarely opened it alone, and he did not open it now. He held it in one hand, the small weight of it in his palm, and he looked at it.

Her mother's locket. Her face inside it. The mother at the laboratory on the morning the experiment failed, a visit with no operational reason, only the reason that she wanted to see her daughter's work. The mother near the configuration when the field expanded. The locket among the debris.

He had found it among debris. He did not remember the finding: this was one of the things that had gone, in the years that took things, and what remained was only that he had always had it. Since the beginning of the beginning, as far as he could reach back. He had believed this was because it had always been his. He had believed this as recently as four hours ago.

He held it. He did not open it.

The face inside it had been looking at him for four months from the outside of the locket. She had known it was there. She had not said it.

He did not know, sitting in the low-ceilinged space with the ambient systems humming around him, what the correct response to this was. He had looked for the correct response and had not found it and understood that the looking was the wrong tool for the job. The correct response was not something he was going to find through assessment. He sat with the locket in his hand and he did not try to find it.

## Beat 3 — The Second Piece

*The only thing that's ever felt like mine.*

He had said this. He had said it in the common room, at the table, in the specific quality of that conversation where he had offered something because the question had been real and the question deserved a real answer. He had said it and he had meant it. It had been the most accurate sentence he had produced about himself in years: the object as proxy for the interior, the only thing he had kept as evidence of something he could not name.

It was not his.

He held this alongside the first piece and looked at both of them. He was in the territory past the tools. He knew the territory: he had been here once before, in the early years, when the career was new enough that he had not yet built the architecture that made the career survivable, and the territory had been vast and without feature. He had built himself out of it by moving. He had moved until the architecture was there, and the architecture had held, and he had not been back.

He was back.

The drive. The career, the calling, the eleven years of extraction work and the specific quality of the rightness he had felt when he was in it: the compass pointing, the work that had felt like the one thing he was made for. He had believed it was his. He had believed it had come from inside him, from whatever he was before the accident had taken the before, from some genuine orientation toward the work that was irreducibly his own.

He had asked the fever question once, in a journal, at the low hour of a night he had not expected to survive. *If the drive was never mine, then neither was the man.*

He had written it and filed it as something he would figure out later. Later was here.

She had caused the accident. The accident had shaped the drive. The drive had shaped the man. And the one object he had kept as evidence that something in him was genuinely his own was hers.

He was not angry. Anger would have been a floor. Anger would have been something to stand on. What he was in was below the floor: the specific groundless condition of someone who has discovered that the things they built their self-understanding on were not what they believed, and has not yet found what the self-understanding should have been built on instead. The misbelief was gone. The truth was not yet there.

He was between them. There was nothing under him.

He set the locket on the equipment rack. He looked at it. He looked at the ceiling twelve inches above his head.

He sat in what was left.

## Beat 4 — The Bottom

He did not contact anyone.

He thought about the journal. He had used it in the early weeks of the Valley: the record of his processing, the externalization of things that needed to be outside his head to be looked at clearly. He reached for it in the way he reached for tools, and then set it down in the same way. He did not have words for this yet. Writing it would require words, and words would require that what he was in had enough shape to be described, and it did not have enough shape. It was not a thing he could write about yet.

He thought about Quill. He thought about Toby. He thought about Nalani, who was not in the Valley tonight.

He did not reach for any of them.

This was not the kind of thing he reached for people for. He had not built that habit. He had built the opposite habit: the one where the interior territory was interior, where what happened inside the architecture stayed inside the architecture because the architecture was the thing that made him operational, and operational was what he needed to be. The habit had served him across eleven years. He was aware, in a distant way, that the habit was not serving him now. He was also aware that the habit was the only one he had.

He sat in the low-ceilinged space and he thought about what Toby had said about carrying. He thought about what Mira had done in the archive research section with one hand at Lena's shoulder. He thought about what Aria-7 had said about a structure that holds something else in place. He had read all of these things and filed them and now the filing had a different weight.

He had been reading the team for months. He had been good at the reading. He had been reading a group of people who were each in their own version of this territory, each carrying something that required carrying, and he had observed it with the specific quality of someone who understood what he was seeing and had not entered it. He had stood on the outside of it. The outside was gone.

The night passed around him. He did not track it precisely. He was aware of the ship's ambient sounds shifting in the small ways that indicated hours had moved, temperature had moved, the Valley settling further into its dark.

He did not sleep.

He sat at the bottom. He had not chosen to be there, and he did not know how to leave, and the bottom was very quiet.

## Beat 5 — The Night

He did sleep eventually. Not a decision: the body's requirement overriding the rest of it, the specific insistence of a system that does not negotiate.

The dream came.

It was the dream he had been having since early in the mission: the woman at the edge of his peripheral vision, the warmth of the compass behind the sternum, the something-approaching that had always resolved into nothing at the end. He knew it as a dream now, mid-dream, in the way of someone who has had a thing enough times to recognize its architecture.

The woman turned.

He had known she was going to turn. He had not known he had known it until she did it. Her face was Lena's face: not the approximation of a familiar face reassembled from dream-material, but precisely hers, the specific quality of her attention when it was fully engaged, the way she looked when she was looking at something she had decided to look at directly. She was looking at him directly.

The compass behind his sternum: present, warm, unchanged.

The something-approaching: approaching.

He stood in the dream and held it and waited for the end of the dream, which had always been nothing. The nothing arrived. It was different. It had always been the nothing of *I don't know what was taken*: the loss without content, the absence without a name. The nothing now was the nothing of *I know exactly what was taken and I know who took it and I do not know yet what to do with either thing.* The content was present. The resolution was not.

He woke before dawn.

The ship was still around him. The locket was on the equipment rack. The Valley outside was the specific dark of an hour before the light begins and before anything has announced that it will.

He looked at the ceiling. He was still at the bottom. He had not, in the night, found a reason to stand. The reasons existed: he was aware they existed, could feel their existence as a fact in the same way he could feel the compass, present and warm and unambiguous. But existing and being usable were different things, and the reasons were not usable yet.

He lay in the low-ceilinged space and waited for the light to arrive and did not try to find anything before it did.

The misbelief was gone. The answer was not yet there. Between them was the space where he was going to have to build something new, and he did not have materials yet, and he knew that the not-having was not permanent, and he was at the bottom, and the bottom held.

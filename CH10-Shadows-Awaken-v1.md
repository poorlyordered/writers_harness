## Chapter Type

Act 1 All Is Lost / Ordinary World Destroyed. Drayton's forces strike the mining colony — targeted, precise, unmistakably deliberate. This is not a raid; it is a statement. Jax fights back with everything he has: eleven years of miner's instinct, the shard responding with something he doesn't have time to examine, pure survival mechanics. Two people are present who will matter: Toby Blackwood, whose ship is what stands between some people and what's coming, and Mira Stavros, whose contract just ended with the attack. The ordinary world is not insufficient anymore. It is gone. The choice is no longer whether to leave.

## Story Circle Beats

**"Pay" phase** — the full cost. Everything Jax was protecting by refusing the call is destroyed in the attack. The refusal is no longer an option because there is no ordinary world to return to. This is not a punishment for the refusal — it is the consequence of the larger forces that Nalani told him are moving, arriving at the colony he has been standing in. The shard's response during the fight is the first glimpse of what Jax's latent Daybringer abilities look like under pressure: something happened that he didn't do consciously, and he does not have time to examine it, and this will matter later.

## POV Character

Jax Tarkin. The attack sequence is action-forward — Jax's combat instincts, his reading of the battlefield, his protection of people around him. Do not slow down for interiority in the middle of the fight. The interiority happens at the chapter's end, after the immediate threat has passed, when he stands in what's left of the ordinary world and understands that the argument for staying is over.

## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — Before the Attack**
Early in the chapter — the last hour of normalcy, though neither Jax nor the reader knows yet. He is doing something ordinary and slightly significant: talking to Toby Blackwood about a cargo run, or checking equipment near where Mira Stavros is on her last day of a security contract. Establish both characters in motion before the attack so their presence during it has organic weight.

**Tobias "Toby" Blackwood:** Transport pilot. DE is one of his regular stops — he has been moving cargo in and out of the colony for years, knows the landing pads and the people and the informal economics of the place. Easy in the way of people who have learned how to be comfortable in uncomfortable places. His ship is nearby because he's between loads; this is unremarkable. He is the kind of person you trust because he has never given you a reason not to, which is different from the kind of person you trust because you understand them.

**Mira Stavros:** Private security. Her contract with a mining outfit — not DE itself but a contractor operating here — ends today. She is wrapping up the end-of-contract logistics: equipment return, final walkthrough, paperwork. She is economical in the way of people who have spent years reading physical threats: she moves with awareness of exits and angles without appearing to. Her contract ending today is narrative timing that will feel earned because Mira is already in the mode of *what's next* when the attack begins.

**Scene 2 — The Attack**
Drayton's forces arrive with the signature Jax heard about in Ch 3 — targeted, fast, knowing exactly where to hit and how. This is not improvised. This is an operation with specific objectives in the colony, and people are in the way of those objectives.

The fight is short and violent and expensive. Jax acts without time for planning: miner's instinct, physical capability, eleven years of surviving things, the immediate calculus of who is most exposed and how to reduce that exposure. He is good at this in the way that surprised people often are — the competence that comes from needing it and not from training.

The shard flares. Not a pulse, not a hum — something that moves through him and out in a burst that accomplishes something (a specific defensive effect: a blast radius that shouldn't have happened, a force that came from him and not from his tools) and leaves him shaken and confused and with no time to process any of it. He registers it as *something happened* and moves forward. He does not stop to examine it. There are still threats.

Toby gets his ship between the people who need cover and what's coming. This is not a heroic gesture — it is a pilot's practical read of what the ship can do and what the situation needs. He does it without being asked. This is who Toby is before he becomes a character choice.

Mira's security training is visible in the attack's immediate aftermath: she has made decisions in the fight that protected people without them knowing they were protected. Her contract ending today did not slow her down. She is already thinking about what comes after in a way that has nothing to do with the contract.

**Scene 3 — After**
The attack ends. Drayton's forces withdraw — they accomplished what they came for, or they determined that continuing was not worth the cost. What they came for was not Jax specifically; he was peripheral. This is important: he was not the target. He is, he will understand, adjacent to the target.

The colony is changed. Not destroyed — but changed in the way that violence changes a place: the geography is the same, the people are the same, but the version of this place that felt stable has been replaced with a version that cannot claim stability.

Jax stands in it. He has the specific stillness of someone who has just come through something and hasn't started running the internal accounting yet. The shard is quiet in his pocket — spent, or waiting, he can't tell.

Toby finds him. Says nothing particularly consequential — something practical about the ship, the situation, what next. But stays.

Mira appears. Her contract is gone with everything else it was attached to. She looks at Jax with the direct gaze of someone who has just made an assessment and arrived at a conclusion. She does not state the conclusion. She stays.

The ordinary world is gone.

*The rug pulled. The choice is no longer whether to leave — it is what kind of leaving.*

## Subplot Threads

- **Toby introduction:** Pilot with history at DE, organic reason to be present and organic reason to leave. His loyalty will be a question for later chapters; right now he is simply competent and present and, critically, he has a ship.
- **Mira introduction:** Security professional whose institutional anchor has just been destroyed. She is already in an adaptive mode that makes her immediately functional in the next situation. Her protection instinct preceded her awareness of who she was protecting.
- **Shard in combat:** Something happened. He doesn't know what. The latent Daybringer ability made its first appearance under pressure — not trained, not understood, not controllable. It will need to be addressed in Ch 14-16.
- **Drayton's operation:** Targeted and purposeful — they were not here for Jax, but the operation that drove them here is connected to everything the Valley told him. The forces he was asked to stand against have arrived at his front door before he made the choice to stand.

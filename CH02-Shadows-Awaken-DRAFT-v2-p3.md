## Scene 3 -- The Dream Returns

The dream came back at 0230.

Not the standard version. Not just the flash and the pain and the nothing. He'd been through enough iterations to know the standard version by its shape, the way it arrived and ran its course and released him back into the dark with his heart doing the thing it did and the ceiling above him unchanged. The standard version had structure. He'd built a relationship with it across eleven years the way you built a relationship with a recurring injury: learned its onset, learned its duration, learned not to expect it to be better this time.

This was different.

The flash came the way it always came. The pain came the way it always came. And then, in the space before the nothing, in the fractional moment between sensation and absence that he'd learned to recognize as the threshold, there was something that hadn't been there before.

A room.

Not clearly. Not held. Walls, maybe, and a light that was warm in a way that had nothing to do with electricity -- amber, gathered, like something burning quietly at a distance -- and a surface at chest height that was level and precise, the edge of it catching the light in a way that implied it had been cut with intention. Cool, he thought, in the fraction before it was gone. Or he thought he thought that. It was there for less than a second. It was gone before any of his processing could reach it. And then the nothing came and the nothing was the same as it always was, and he woke with his heart doing something inconvenient.

He lay still.

The ceiling of his quarters was pale concrete, cracked at the diagonal he'd tracked across eleven years. The air was the air it always was. He lay still and let his heart work through whatever it needed to work through, because he had learned that arguing with the heartbeat didn't help and moving too fast made the floor feel wrong.

He did not open the tin box. He thought about it. The thought was brief and he set it aside with the same motion he used for everything he wasn't going to act on: noted, filed, not pursued.

He stared at the ceiling.

Eleven years of the dream. The same dream, the same shape, the same nothing waiting at the end of it. He had catalogued it the way a miner catalogued a formation: flash, pain, nothing. Onset predictable within a window. Duration consistent. Aftermath managed. He had learned its edges the way you learned the edges of a space you had to move through carefully, and he had built his sleeping life around the fact of it the way DE's founders had built the town around the mine's constraints.

A room. Not a face, not a voice, not anything that carried narrative weight. Just walls, light, a surface. Something that might have been familiar and might have been invention and was gone too fast to distinguish between the two. He'd had eleven years of the dream and this was the first time it had shown him anything other than the flash and the nothing. A fractional second of something that existed before the nothing arrived.

He did not know whether to call that progress. He did not know what the word would even mean here.

After a while -- ten minutes, maybe twelve -- his heart returned to something he recognized as normal. He got up, because lying still had run its course and getting up was what came next.

He filled a glass of water, drank it at the window, watched the mine head's running lights in the dark outside. They didn't go out. They never went out; the mine ran on a twenty-three-hour cycle and this hour was part of it. The most reliable thing on Desolation's Edge. He'd been watching them since his first week here and they hadn't changed.

He put the glass down. He went back to bed.

He did not sleep well. In the morning he wrote in his journal: *The dream again. Something new. Don't know what to make of it yet.* He looked at the entry for a moment, then closed the journal and went to work.

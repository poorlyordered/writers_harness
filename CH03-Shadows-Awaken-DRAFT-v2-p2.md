## Scene 2 -- The Shard Pulls

His assignment took him into a sector he'd run twice before: a lateral extension off the eastern survey corridor, close enough to the missing crew's last-known position that on a quiet day he could have flagged their operation on his scanner's regional display.

He checked. The flag wasn't there.

He put the scanner to work.

He'd put the shard in his pocket that morning without examining why. It had stayed in his hand longer than usual when he was packing his kit, and when he'd closed the tin he'd slipped it into his pocket rather than back inside. He'd noted this and let it stand. Eleven years of doing what the shard seemed to ask for, including the morning it had seemed to ask to travel closer to him than the tin box allowed.

The shift was methodical. Two stress concentrations in the upper face, an interesting lateral stratification worth flagging for follow-up. The work required enough attention to keep the mind from wandering. He let it require that.

Midway through a pass along the eastern wall, the shard pulsed.

Not a hum. Not the sustained low frequency from the past two mornings. This was shorter and more forceful -- a single beat through his palm and up his wrist before he'd registered what it was. He went still.

The shard pulsed again.

He took it out of his pocket and held it flat on his palm. The shaft around him was unchanged: pale fractured geology, the distant conveyors, the regular mechanical register of an operation running the way it was supposed to run. His scanner registered nothing anomalous.

A third pulse. Oriented -- pointed at something specific, the same directional quality he'd felt in his quarters, but stronger now. He turned slowly, keeping his hand level. The pulse strengthened as he faced northeast. Toward the sector where the crew had last reported.

He stood there for a moment.

He knew what the sensible response was. He had work to do. He had a survey pass half-finished and annotations to log and a stratification worth flagging. The sensible response was to put the shard back in his pocket, pick up his scanner, and complete the pass. He had done the sensible thing with the shard for eleven years. He had filed the inexplicable and moved on from it and done the work in front of him, and eleven years of that had left him with good survey logs and a piece of mineral he could not explain and a dream that had just shown him a room he didn't recognize.

He picked up his tools and went northeast.

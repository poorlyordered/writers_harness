## Chapter Type

Antagonist Introduction (Indirect) / Threat Establishment. Drayton is not present — he arrives through the evidence of his operation. A mining crew is missing. A competitor's extraction site has been shut down in a way that makes "violent" the only honest word for it. Jax hears this secondhand, through the colony's informal communication network. The shard's pulse near the site of the disappearance is the chapter's final beat: something in Jax's own pocket is responding to whatever Drayton's operation is doing, and he does not yet know what to do with that.

## Story Circle Beats

**"Go" phase** — the external threat enters the world Jax has been living in. He does not yet understand its shape or its specific connection to him, but the scale of it is legible enough to register. The shard's response is the chapter's turn: Drayton and the shard are connected in a way Jax cannot explain and does not have a framework for. His reaction is to notice it and keep moving. This is correct and it is the problem.

## POV Character

Jax Tarkin. His processing of the threat information is practical and alert — not afraid, not dismissive, but reading for operational data the way a miner reads formations. What he cannot read is the significance of the shard's response, because he does not yet have the category for it.

## Scene Beats

**Scene 1 — News Moves Through the Colony**
The colony's informal channels — the cafeteria, the equipment bay, the queue for shift assignment — carry the news the way frontier colonies carry news: incompletely, urgently, with the embellishment of people who are scared and are trying not to be. A mining crew working a claim three sectors over has not reported in. Their last comms log shows nothing unusual. The equipment pinged and then didn't.

A second piece: a competitor's extraction operation, smaller than DE and less established, has been shut down. The people who came from there to DE tell the story in the clipped language of people who have processed a shock and emerged with a single overwhelming fact: it was not an accident. It was targeted. It was fast. Whatever did it knew what it was doing.

Jax listens without asking questions. He is building a picture. The picture is not complete but the shape of it is clear enough: someone is moving through the region with purpose and the purpose is not compatible with other operations continuing to exist.

**Scene 2 — Working Near the Disappearance Site**
Jax's regular shift takes him into a sector adjacent to where the missing crew was working. He goes — routine, because routine is how you function when things are not routine. He does his work. He is more alert than usual in a way he does not name as fear.

Midway through the shift, the shard pulses. Not a hum — a pulse, shorter and more forceful than anything it has done before. He stops work, takes the shard out of his pocket where he has carried it since this morning for reasons he hasn't examined. Holds it. It pulses again, oriented in the direction of the missing crew's last-known location.

He stands still for a long moment. Then he replaces the shard, picks up his tool, and finishes the shift.

**Scene 3 — End of Shift / Processing**
Walking back through the colony. The Keeper Quarter. The usual passage without stopping — but this time he slows for a moment. One of the Keeper Quarter residents is outside, doing something ordinary: repair work on a door frame, or tending the small plantings that mark this cluster of buildings as different from the rest of DE. The resident looks up, makes brief eye contact, and returns to work. No greeting. No acknowledgment beyond the fact of the look.

Jax keeps walking. In his quarters: the tin box on the shelf. He opens it, places the shard inside, closes it. The shard is quiet now — as if the moment of response is over.

He sits with this for about thirty seconds. He has work to do tomorrow. That is the relevant fact.

*The shard has demonstrated connection to whatever Drayton's operation is doing. Jax has noticed. He has not yet formulated the question.*

## Subplot Threads

- **Drayton introduction:** Present only through effect — missing people, a destroyed operation, the signature of a purposeful intelligence working at scale. He is not a name yet; he is a force the reader learns to fear before they learn to hate.
- **Shard escalation:** From hum (Ch 1) to directional response (Ch 2) to pulse (Ch 3). Each escalation is slightly more forceful. The shard is a receiver and the signal is getting stronger.
- **Keeper Quarter:** Second pass — the look from a resident. Still not a conversation. The residents are tracking his proximity to the larger story without engaging it directly.
